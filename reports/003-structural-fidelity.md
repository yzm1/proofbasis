## 1. Independently checked reference fixture

### 1.1 Source calculus and equality

Fix cut-free, unit-free, one-sided classical multiplicative linear logic, **without MIX**, with distinct atoms `p,r,q`, atomic axioms, and formula occurrences tracked through each rule. Formulas are generated by literals, tensor `⊗`, and par `⅋`. Contexts are finite unordered forests; exchange is implicit, not an extra proof event. Tensor arguments are ordered. The rules are:

```text
             ax             ⊢ Γ,A       ⊢ B,Δ               ⊢ Γ,A,B
       ⊢ a⊥,a              ------------------ ⊗            ---------- ⅋
                              ⊢ Γ,A⊗B,Δ                     ⊢ Γ,A⅋B
```

A net consists of the concluding formula forest and atomic axiom matching. Occurrences are identified by their final tree positions, not merely atom names. Source equality is identical matching on this fixed forest. This is also the rule-commutation quotient: van Glabbeek–Hughes Theorem 1 proves both directions for cut-free MALL without units, with or without MIX; the multiplicative subfragment applies here. It does not assert equality of arbitrary cut-containing proofs after normalization. [VERIFIED_SOURCE, S1]

Let `P=p⊗r`, `T=P⊗q`, and `X=p⊥⅋r⊥`. From the three atomic axioms define:

```text
     ap: ⊢ p⊥,p        ar: ⊢ r⊥,r
     -------------------------------- ⊗i
     D: ⊢ p⊥,r⊥,P

     D: ⊢ p⊥,r⊥,P
     -------------------- ⅋b
          ⊢ X,P                    aq: ⊢ q,q⊥
     --------------------------------------- ⊗o
     d₁: ⊢ X,T,q⊥

     D: ⊢ p⊥,r⊥,P                  aq: ⊢ q,q⊥
     --------------------------------------- ⊗o
                  ⊢ p⊥,r⊥,T,q⊥
     --------------------------------------- ⅋b
     d₂: ⊢ X,T,q⊥
```

Every tensor splits its premise resources. In `d₁`, the outer tensor acts on `P` and `q`; `X` is context. In `d₂`, par acts on the two negative context occurrences; it does not act on either principal tensor input. Thus this is a legal heterogeneous rule commutation, not an illicit distributive law. Both proofs contain precisely three axioms, two tensors, and one par. [INFERENCE]

### 1.2 Same net, including correctness

Give the final leaves names `p⁻,r⁻` inside `X`; `p⁺,r⁺` inside `P`; `q⁺` inside `T`; and the separate `q⁻`. Both derivations induce exactly:

```text
axiom edges: (p⁻,p⁺), (r⁻,r⁺), (q⁻,q⁺)
formula edges: X--p⁻, X--r⁻, P--p⁺, P--r⁺, T--P, T--q⁺
```

That is literally the same labeled graph. There is one par node, hence two switchings. Each removes one of the edges from `X`. In either switching the remaining graph has nine vertices, eight edges, and is connected: the tensor subtree connects all positive leaves, each negative leaf attaches by its axiom, and `X` attaches to one negative leaf. Hence each switching is a tree. The net is correct under the switching criterion; no MIX, units, boxes, or additive choices enter this check. Sequentialization is already witnessed by the two displayed derivations. [INFERENCE; criterion independently checked in S2 §6.1 and S14]

### 1.3 Precisely which LF encoding fails

PR #1 specifies raw sequent-rule constructors and LF conversion schematically, rather than a complete occurrence-handling signature. Here is an explicit instance of that proposed encoding class. Fix **ordinary LF with βη conversion**, no added object equations or rewrite rules, and a deep explicit-sequent encoding. To make list bookkeeping checkable, use finite formula/list constructors and these type families:

```text
fm : type                       ctx : type
nil : ctx                       cons : fm → ctx → ctx
tenf, parf : fm → fm → fm        prf : ctx → type
take : fm → ctx → ctx → type
weave : ctx → ctx → ctx → type
```

Add `atom:type` and `pos,neg:atom→fm`. `take A Γ Ω` removes one specified occurrence of `A`; its constructors are `take-head` and `take-tail`. `weave Γ Δ Ω` interleaves the lists; its constructors are `weave-empty`, `weave-left`, and `weave-right`. All are LF constants, with the indexed types expanded in the [fixture source notes](003-source-notes.md#lf-fixture-complete-administrative-evidence). For example, `take-head : take A (cons A Γ) Γ` and `take-tail : take A Γ Ω → take A (cons B Γ) (cons B Ω)`. No relation computes by conversion.

The proof constructors, with implicit parameters expanded as LF Π-binders, are:

```text
ax : Πa:atom. prf [neg a,pos a]

tensor : take A Γ Γ₀ → take B Δ Δ₀ → weave Γ₀ Δ₀ Ω
       → take (tenf A B) Ξ Ω → prf Γ → prf Δ → prf Ξ

par : take A Γ Γ₁ → take B Γ₁ Ω
    → take (parf A B) Ξ Ω → prf Γ → prf Ξ
```

The last `take` specifies insertion of the principal formula in the concluding list. This avoids quietly imposing AC equality on LF lists or adding administrative exchange roots. Each displayed rule instance has finite `take`/`weave` evidence, supplied explicitly in the notes. Choose `D`'s list `[p⊥,r⊥,P]`, the third axiom's representative `[q⊥,q]`, and final list `[X,T,q⊥]`; insert `T` after `X` for `d₁` and between `r⊥` and `q⊥` before the final par for `d₂`. The schemas type both translations at exactly `prf [X,T,q⊥]`. No full adequacy theorem for this auxiliary signature is assumed or needed for the unequal pair.

Write `κ` for the explicitly supplied selection/interleaving arguments, distinguished for each rule instance. Their values are constructor terms, not equality proofs. The proof terms are:

```text
u  = tensor κi axp axr
t₁ = tensor κo₁ (par κb₁ u) axq
t₂ = par κb₂ (tensor κo₂ u axq)
```

Both are fully applied, closed terms of an atomic LF family, with no β-redex; any η-expansion of functional arguments leaves their outer heads unchanged. Their heads are respectively the distinct uninterpreted constants `tensor` and `par`. Uniqueness of LF normal forms therefore gives `t₁ ≠βη t₂`. In β-only LF they also differ. These constants encode rules; LF conversion does not permute applications of different rule constants. [INFERENCE; LF normalization/conversion and canonical forms: S5]

**Exact conclusion:** `d₁ ~net d₂` but `F(d₁) ≠LF F(d₂)`. This encoding **fails preservation** of the chosen equality. Assuming a raw-proof injection, it reflects equality automatically; injection is not the missing property. Any βη-only LF encoding with these two distinct canonical heads fails on this pair, but this conditional observation does not cover other signatures, shallow encodings, canonical-net translations, explicit equality judgments, or CLF's monadic equality. [INFERENCE]

### 1.4 Negative controls

**Distinct nets of the same sequent.** At the occurrence-labeled forest `⊢ p₁⊥,p₂⊥,p₃⊗p₄`, compare matchings `{(1,3),(2,4)}` and `{(1,4),(2,3)}`. Both have atomic-axiom/tensor derivations and tree-shaped switching graphs, but the matchings differ. They are unequal under the selected net quotient. A target identifying them violates reflection. Occurrence labels are essential even though every atom is named `p`. [INFERENCE]

**Forbidden duplication.** In multiplicative intuitionistic linear logic, `p ⊢ p⊗p` is underivable: tensor introduction requires two occurrences and there is only one. Atom balance also refutes the corresponding classical sequent `⊢ p⊥,p⊗p`. An ordinary cartesian pairing translation admits `x ↦ (x,x)` and cannot reflect linear derivability. This says nothing against explicit-context LF encodings or native linear contexts. [INFERENCE; S5–S6]

These controls reject both indiscriminate proof identification and unrestricted assumption reuse. Passing this small fixture proves no universal property.

## 2. What structural fidelity would have to mean

The following is a **proposed specification**, not a charter amendment or existence theorem. [CONJECTURE as a class-wide target]

Fix independently a class of **presented pairs `(S,~S)`**, where `~S` is a typed congruence on proofs. For binding/dependent systems, use an indexed family over contexts and well-typed substitutions, rather than force everything into a single untyped graph. Fix the target's typing, equality, admissible extensions, and global certificate policy before examining examples. Count its typing/equality rules and parameter interpretation among its finite schemas; a finite list of constructor names alone is insufficient.

For each translated boundary `J`, require:

1. **Judgments:** source inhabitation iff target inhabitation at `T(J)`, quantifying over all target inhabitants in the specified interface, not only images of source proofs.
2. **Composition:** identities, cut/grafting, and term/proof substitution commute with translation modulo target equality. Where the objects are cut-free, composition requires cut followed by normalization or an independently justified graph composition; tree grafting leaves the domain.
3. **Equality preservation:** `d ~S e ⇒ F(d) ≡A F(e)`.
4. **Equality reflection:** `F(d) ≡A F(e) ⇒ d ~S e`.
5. **Fullness, when claimed:** every target proof at a translated boundary is equal to some `F(d)`. This is stronger than existence reflection. Preservation lets `F` descend to proof classes; reflection makes that map injective; fullness makes it surjective.
6. **Resources:** a typed map on assumption occurrences and rule ports preserves splitting, multiplicity, binding, and authorized deletion/copying. Specify whether linear, affine, relevant, cartesian, ordered, or modal. A source inference constant and a definition carry different trust obligations.
7. **Events, when claimed:** specify events, enabling, dependence, independence, and conflict independently of the encoder. Supply labeled residual maps for steps that copy or erase events. A fixed event bijection is appropriate for pure permutations, not arbitrary β-reduction.

For a categorical source, items 2–5 are a fully faithful structured functor on the selected hom-sets; essential surjectivity is an additional claim. For multiple-conclusion linear proofs, a polycategory can retain the boundaries more directly. For open derivations, substitute in all premise holes: a bijection only on closed theorems does not settle contextual composition.

The source class must not be “all systems embeddable in A.” A finite benchmark can always invite a disjoint union of its calculi; it is a test, not proof of a common basis. The description of admissible structural parameters needs a Task-002 anti-vacuity criterion. This report does not redefine that other assignment or assert that structure-preservation alone excludes every disguised interpreter.

### Correction of PR #2's lemma

Let `F:D→T` be injective and target equality on its image be syntactic identity. Then:

```text
F(d) ≡ F(e) ⇔ d=e.
```

For any equivalence `~` on `D`, **reflection always holds**, since `d=e ⇒ d~e`. Preservation holds iff `~` identifies no distinct elements. PR #2's original “reflects only if” swaps these two directions; its self-review is correct. The MLL pair exhibits the failed preservation half. [INFERENCE]

Conversely, a map sending every proof of a boundary to one target token preserves every source equality and fails reflection whenever two source classes differ. Adding source equations to a target makes preservation easier, but **does not make reflection automatic**: interactions with other target equations can collapse extra pairs. Declared equality judgments can represent evidence of `d~e` adequately without making `F(d)` and `F(e)` definitionally equal. These are different contracts. Neither the blanket “extensions imply vacuity” claim nor the alleged exhaustive “interpreter or union” dichotomy is justified. [INFERENCE; positive counterweights S7–S10]

## 4. Resources, causality, conflict, and generations

### 4.1 Duplication and erasure must be authorized operations

In the tensor-only linear fragment, assumption occurrences must be used exactly once; affine structure additionally permits dropping, relevant structure copying, unrestricted structure both. Other connectives can authorize their own context handling, which must be stated rather than inferred from this fragment. Exchange and associativity are additional structural choices. Tensor splitting is not cartesian pairing. For exponentials, contraction can authorize copies of a boxed computation; weakening can erase it. “The same formula occurred twice” does not record whether there were two initial resources or an authorized copy. [INFERENCE; resource disciplines in S3/S6/S11]

A resource specification should give each rule its input/output occurrence map, its modality, and its copy/erase authorization; composition must transport these maps. Labels expose resource provenance but can impose a **finer** identity than a source with indistinguishable tokens. CLF II explicitly chooses individual-token semantics (pp.27–28). In the collective-token setting, consuming either of two identical tokens may count as the same behavior; in this CLF encoding it counts as a different choice. That is a source-identity mismatch to disclose, not a defect cured by calling labels administrative. [INFERENCE supported by VERIFIED_SOURCE S3]

Full faithfulness on a chosen quotient need not preserve every observable resource history. In cartesian βη syntax, `(λx.z) M` equals `z`, erasing the construction of `M`; `(λx.(x,x)) M` equals `(M,M)`, duplicating its occurrences. A bijection of all raw events cannot survive such identities without an erasure/copy residual convention or additional higher-dimensional witnesses. This is a conditional incompatibility between that R4 demand and that R3 quotient, not an impossibility of faithful categorical semantics. [INFERENCE]

### 4.2 Causal precedence differs from rule-tree ancestry

In the fixture, once `D` and `aq` are prepared, `⅋b` uses the negative leaves and `⊗o` uses `P,q`. Neither operation uses the principal formula produced by the other. Both application orders reach the same boundary and net. The path through a sequent tree orders them differently, but that ancestry is not invariant under the selected proof equality. A proposed R4 preserving every raw-tree ancestor relation would conflict with this R3 requirement. [INFERENCE]

This local diamond must not be promoted into an assertion that **every** topological ordering of net links is a legal sequent proof. Even the inner tensor and par use disjoint principal leaves, yet applying par before the inner tensor would require joining two separate axiom-premise contexts. Without MIX there is no such step in this fixture. Link data flow alone omits context co-residence and global correctness. Existing sequentialization and switching criteria supply the missing mathematical obligations; a scheduler does not. [INFERENCE; S1–S2/S14]

For a finite typed construction trace, define dependence by production-to-use edges on labeled resources. Legal independent adjacent steps must satisfy a residual diamond: both orders are enabled, have corresponding outputs, and induce the chosen equality. CLF II §5.2.1 checks both token overlap and producer/consumer dependencies. An independence relation on **rule names alone** is too crude: two instances of the same rule can use disjoint resources or compete for one token. [VERIFIED_SOURCE S3; INFERENCE application]

### 4.3 Explicit conflict and nonconfluence witness

Consider an individual-token system with initial `x:a` and two transitions `b : a → b` and `c : a → c`, with distinct terminal places `b,c`. The two enabled transition instances both consume `x`, so neither sequence `bc` nor `cb` is valid. They are competing events in conflict, not independent reorderings. Their resulting markings differ and have no successors: this is a nonjoinable peak. A partial order with two unrelated events wrongly allows the configuration containing both; an event structure adds `b # c`. [INFERENCE; S13 for the definition]

For contrast, transitions acting on `x:a` and `y:d`, with fresh disjoint outputs, commute. The two sequences are different executions, equal trace classes, and equal CLF expressions under the verified encoding. In canonical CLF notation the equation has shape:

```text
let {u}=b x in let {v}=e y in (u,v)
  =c
let {v}=e y in let {u}=b x in (u,v).
```

Both operands use exactly their own linear token. Replace `e y` by an operation using `u` and this equality rule no longer applies. Replace `y` by the same consumed `x` and the combined trace is ill-typed. [INFERENCE; S3]

A free category may contain distinct paths through a nonconfluent peak. Nonconfluence does not invalidate such a categorical representation. It threatens a proposed **normal-form characterization** of equality or a typed rewriting theory's metatheory; those are separate claims.

### 4.4 Generational semantics has no uniform definition yet

For finite acyclic occurrence traces, one reasonable derived generation rank is `g(e)=0` for minimal events and `g(e)=1+max g(e′)` over immediate causes otherwise. Resources record their producing event; initial tokens precede generation zero. Equal labeled traces have the same rank after label transport. Execution step numbers can differ between two schedules and are not these ranks. This is an explicitly proposed interpretation of “generational,” not a published cross-foundation fidelity theorem. [INFERENCE]

Rank loses information: different causal graphs can have the same layers; conflict is not encoded by rank. Copying/erasure under reduction needs parent-to-residual provenance, not just integers. A finite cyclic preproof has no ordinary acyclic rank; its unfolding has occurrence events, while the finite graph reuses nodes. This requires a separate unfolding/progress semantics. No inspected framework establishes one common generational invariant over all these objects. [UNKNOWN as a uniform theorem]

## 5. Global validity, trust, costs, and corrected negative claims

**Graph validity.** The fixture's two switching checks are explicit, but the rule “all switchings connected and acyclic” is global. A locally well-typed graph can fail it. The cyclic analogue is a quantified condition: every infinite path has a tail supporting an infinitely progressing trace. For example, a structural contraction/weakening loop from `P⊢Q` back to itself has legitimate local steps but no unfolding/progress; with `P` true and `Q` false its conclusion is invalid. It is only a preproof. [INFERENCE; S15]

A sound representation can use validated whole objects, preserve global correctness through proved operations, or attach explicit validity certificates. Their completeness and cost are new obligations. Undecidable acceptance of some **bare** preproofs does not forbid decidable checking of augmented finite witnesses for an r.e. criterion. A hypothetical polynomial-size witness theorem for a PSPACE-complete criterion would need `NP=PSPACE`; that conditional cost statement does not rule out larger witnesses or other certificates.

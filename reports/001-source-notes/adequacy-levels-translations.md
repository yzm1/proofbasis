## S2. Miller & Pimentel, "A formal framework for specifying sequent calculus proof systems"
- Bib (as printed in retrieved PDF's ref list / web listing): Dale Miller, Elaine Pimentel. Theoretical Computer Science 474 (2013) 98–116 (volume/pages from web search result; NOT printed in retrieved preprint).
- URL retrieved: https://www.lix.polytechnique.fr/~dale/papers/Llinda.pdf  — author PREPRINT (Elsevier-style manuscript, no DOI printed). Page numbers below = preprint pages.
- Status: VERIFIED_SOURCE (text read).

### Adequacy definition (Sec. 3.4, preprint p.11), verbatim
"Following [27], we will identify three levels of adequacy of specifications. The least restrictive level is based on relative completeness: that is, at this level, the set of provable sequents is the same – an object-level sequent has a proof if and only if the corresponding meta-level sequent has a proof. The second level of adequacy is based on full completeness of proofs: that is, the proofs of an object-level sequent are in one-to-one correspondence with the meta-level proofs of the specifications of the object sequent. Finally, the most restrictive level of adequacy is based on full completeness of derivations: that is, the object-level derivations (partial proofs, such as inference rules themselves) are in one-to-one correspondence."
[27] = V. Nigam, D. Miller, A framework for proof systems, J. of Automated Reasoning 45(2) (2010) 157–188.
Note: "Focusing is the critical device that allows us to state and prove such adequacy results." Meta-level proofs here = FOCUSED (LLF) proofs; the bijection is with focused proofs, not with arbitrary LL proofs (paraphrase/inference from Sec. 2–3; Theorem 1 = Andreoli completeness of focusing).

### Theorem 6 (p.13) verbatim
"The sequent −→ B has a linear logic proof if and only if the sequent LL, Cut, Init; ⌈B⌉ ⇑ · is provable in linear logic; has an LK proof if and only if LK, Cut, Init, Pos, Neg; ⌈B⌉ ⇑ · is provable in linear logic; and has an LM proof if and only if LM, Cut, Init, Pos; ⌈B⌉ ⇑ · is provable in linear logic."
Following text: "While this proposition states that these proof system specifications are adequate at the level of relative completeness ..., the direct proofs of these statements easily show that the actual adequacy result is at the level of full completeness of proofs. As the next example illustrates, adequacy at the level of full completeness of derivations is not achieved by these specifications".
- Example 7 (LM): ⊃L spec focuses to two different object rules, one not legal in LM -> NOT full completeness of derivations ("full adequacy for proofs does hold for LM").
- Example 8 (LJ = LM + units fi, ti): adequacy "only at the level of 'relative completeness'" (footnote: "the proof of such a proposition is not by simple structural induction"); a meta-proof of ⌈(ti⊃fi)⊃A⌉ "does not have any correspondent in LJ". Fix: change ⊃L and Cut clauses to use ! on the right premise ((⊃L) ⌊A⊃B⌋⊥ ⊗ (!⌈A⌉ ⊗ ⌊B⌋); (Cut) ⌊B⌋ ⊗ !⌈B⌉) "to achieve adequacy at the level of full completeness of derivations".
  => The adequacy level is a property of a particular ENCODING, not of the framework; small changes of clause shape move it between levels.

### What lives in the theory (environment) vs. the fixed framework
- Fixed: LLF (Andreoli focused one-sided classical LL), meta-predicates ⌊·⌋ (left) ⌈·⌉ (right).
- In the THEORY (environment): introduction clauses (Figs 2–4), AND Cut, Init, and the structural formulas Pos = ∃B(⌊B⌋⊥ ⊗ ?⌊B⌋), Neg = ∃B(⌈B⌉⊥ ⊗ ?⌈B⌉) (Fig. 5). "the distinction between the intuitionistic (LJ) and classical (LK) proof systems is that the former assumes Pos while the latter assumes both Pos and Neg." (p.15). So structural rules AND cut are specified as theory formulas, i.e., in the environment.
- Restricted shape of rules: Definition 4 "introduction clause is a closed bipole formula" of fixed shape; this syntactic restriction is what blocks arbitrary-interpreter clauses (inference).
### Decidability / meta-theory results (verbatim fragments)
- Prop 16 (p.~19): derivability of an inference rule from a (cut-free) proof system theory decidable by bounded search (abstract: "derivability of an inference rule from a set of inference rules can be decided by bounded (linear) logic programming search on the specified rules").
- Def 18: "A canonical proof system theory is called cut-coherent if all object-level connectives have cut-coherent introduction rules" (cut-coherent: "Cut; · ⇑ ∀x̄(Fl⊥ ⅋ Fr⊥) is provable in LLF").
- Prop 20/21: for cut-coherent X, cut reduces to atomic cut (ACut) and atomic cut is eliminable.
- Theorem 22: "Determining whether or not a canonical proof system is cut-coherent is decidable ... by proof search in LLF bounded by the depth v + 3".
### Stated limitations (verbatim)
- p.~10: "non-commutative logics and their sequent calculus, which use lists as contexts, cannot be described in the framework we have described here. Similarly, the exponentials used in light linear logics [23] do not fit our framework." (subexponentials suggested as extension).
- Sec 3.5: "proof systems containing non-commutative connectives or modal operators probably cannot be captured."
- Sec 4.2: "it is impossible to specify IIL∗ in our setting using only 'one-headed' clauses since it is necessary to have two different linear contexts".
### Proof identity
- Only the three adequacy levels; NO equational theory / proof-identity congruence (cut-elimination equations, permutations) is claimed preserved/reflected. "One-to-one correspondence" of proofs is of object proofs with FOCUSED meta-proofs; focusing quotients away some rule permutations (inference: focused proofs are canonical representatives of permutation classes only partially).
### Relevance (INFERENCE)
- F01: this is the strongest close prior art for "fixed framework + theory environment, derivability preserved and reflected", but scope is restricted to commutative sequent calculi with set/multiset contexts; explicit non-coverage of non-commutative, light, (some) modal systems. Does not address R3/R4.
- F03: Cut, Init, structural rules are TRUSTED theory clauses in the environment; the bipole/introduction-clause shape restriction is the guard against vacuous encodings.
- F08: adequacy level is encoding-dependent (Ex. 7/8): same logic, different clause shapes, different adequacy level.

## S1. Nigam & Miller, "A framework for proof systems"
- Bib: Vivek Nigam, Dale Miller. J. Automated Reasoning 45(2) (2010) 157–188 (bib data as cited in ref [27] of Miller–Pimentel preprint; journal DOI NOT seen).
- URL retrieved: https://www.lix.polytechnique.fr/~dale/papers/nigam-jar.pdf — "Noname manuscript" = author PREPRINT of the JAR paper (states "extended and improved version of the conference paper [Nigam and Miller, 2008a]"). Page numbers = preprint pages.
- Status: VERIFIED_SOURCE.

### Adequacy levels (Sec. 2.3, p.7) verbatim
"The weakest level of adequacy is relative completeness which considers only provability: a formula has a proof in one system if it has a proof in another system. A stronger level of adequacy is of full completeness of proofs: the proofs of a given formula are in one-to-one correspondence with proofs in another system. If one uses the term 'derivation' for possibly incomplete proofs (proofs that may have open premises), we can consider a even stronger level of adequacy. We use the term full completeness of derivations if the derivations (such as inference rules themselves) in one system are in one-to-one correspondence with those in another system."
- Context: inspired by "Girard in [Girard, 2006, Chapter 7] ... three levels of adequacy based on semantical notions: the level of truth, the level of functions, and the level of actions."
- Note (INFERENCE): the definition gives no formal statement of what "one-to-one correspondence" must respect (no compositionality / substitution requirement stated in the definition; no proof-equality congruence). Meta-proofs are LLF focused proofs, so the bijection is with focused proofs under a fixed POLARITY ASSIGNMENT for meta-atoms.
- Intro (p.2): "All the proof systems that we shall encode will be done with this third, most refined level of adequacy." and "The adequacy of a given specification of inference rules requires first assigning polarity to meta-level atoms ...: then adequacy is generally an immediate consequence of the focusing theorem of linear logic."
- p.2: "while provability is not affected, different meta-level focused proofs are built and these encode different object-level proof systems." (Same theory, different polarity => different object proof system; derivability identical.)

### Which encodings reach which level (verbatim statements of propositions)
- Prop 2 (p.11): with all meta-atoms negative, "1) Γ ⊢lm C iff ⊢llf Llm, ⌊Γ⌋ : ⌈C⌉ ⇑ 2) Γ ⊢lj C iff ⊢llf Llj, ⌊Γ⌋ : ⌈C⌉ ⇑ 3) Γ ⊢lk ∆ iff ⊢llf Llk, ⌊Γ⌋, ⌈∆⌉ : · ⇑. Furthermore, adequacy for derivations also holds between the respective proof systems." (LM/LJ/LK sequent calculi with cut: full completeness of derivations.) Proof is only sketched ("one shows that focusing (deciding) on formulas within the linear logic theories ... encodes exactly the corresponding sequent calculus inference rule").
- Prop 3: cut-free variants (drop Id2) — same, derivation-level.
- Props 4–6: relating the modular theories (L, Id1, Id2, StrL, StrR) to Llk etc.: "The equivalences that appear in the following three propositions are all at the most shallow level of adequacy: the equivalence of provability."
- Prop 7 (p.15): NJ natural deduction, ⌊·⌋ positive, ⌈·⌉ negative: Γ ⊢nj C iff ⊢llf Lnj, ⌊Γ⌋ : ⌈C⌉ ⇑; normal NJ (no ∨, ∃) via Lfnj. ("In order for this encoding to be adequate at the level of derivations, we simply change the polarity assignment".) NB: normal-proof result restricted to formulas without ∨ and ∃; negation/falsity needs extra formulas [Nigam & Miller 2008b].
- Prop 8: LJ vs NJ — "relative completeness result" (provability only).
- Props 9,13 (generalized elimination GE, generalized intro), 15 (Free Deduction), 17 (KE tableaux), 19 (Smullyan analytic cut AC): each "Furthermore, adequacy for derivations also holds" (as stated). Props 10,14,16,18,20/Cor 6: relationships to LK etc. only at provability level.
- Sec 2.3/p.~9: "one might still be interested in theories that are adequate only at the level of (complete) proofs. For example, following the Curry-Howard isomorphism, functional programs are complete proofs and their execution involve the removal of (some) cut rules".

### Environment vs framework
- Fixed: LLF + polarity assignment. Theory (environment): introduction clauses, Id1/Id2 (initial/cut), StrL/StrR (structural rules via ?), with structural rules later "incorporated into introduction rules" by adding ?/! (p.7, step 2: "they do not provide a good focusing behavior since the equivalences they imply can yield loops").
- So structural rules and cut are TRUSTED environment formulas. Polarity assignment is an additional, essential, out-of-theory parameter.

### Negative results / limitations (Sec. 9, p.24–25) verbatim
"non-commutative logics, such as [Lambek, 1958, Abrusci and Ruet, 1999], and proof systems based on hypersequents [Avron, 1991] do not appear to be captured by direct encodings into linear logic. Also, the inference rules for the 'hybrid' conjunction ... analyzed by Hughes [2005] does not seem possible to treat". And: "while we do not believe it is possible to naturally encode object-level focused proof systems, such as LJT and LJQ, or multi-conclusion proof systems for intuitionistic logic [Maehara, 1954] within linear logic, they can be encoded in versions of linear logic extended with appropriate subexponentials (see Chapter 6 of [Nigam, 2009])." These are stated as beliefs/appearances, not impossibility theorems.

### Proof identity
- Not addressed beyond bijections of (open) derivations. No equational theory (cut-reduction, η, commuting conversions) is shown preserved/reflected. Bijection of derivations is the strongest "identity" notion: syntactic identity of derivation trees.

### Relevance (INFERENCE)
- F01: For a fixed list of commutative sequent/ND/tableaux systems, this realises "fixed meta-logic + environment theory" with derivation-level bijection. But: not a single theorem quantifying over a class of logics (each Prop proved separately; Miller-Pimentel's "canonical/bipole" class gives a uniform class for derivability/cut-coherence). Explicit gaps: non-commutative, hypersequent, hybrid, LJT/LJQ.
- F03/F08: Adequacy depends on polarity assignment + clause shape; trusted structural/cut clauses in environment.
- F04: no proof-identity congruence; "1-1 derivations" is identity on trees, which is incompatible with quotienting by permutations unless both sides quotient compatibly.

## S4b. Selinger, "Control categories and duality: on the categorical semantics of the lambda-mu calculus"
- Bib (printed on retrieved PDF): Peter Selinger. Math. Struct. in Comp. Science (2001), vol. 11, pp. 207–260, Cambridge University Press. "Received 12 September 1998; revised 2 November 1999". DOI not printed.
- URL retrieved: https://www.mathstat.dal.ca/~selinger/papers/control.pdf — author copy with journal header (page numbering is the author copy's own running numbers, e.g. "Peter Selinger 30"; may differ from journal pages). Status: VERIFIED_SOURCE.
### Key statements (verbatim)
- Def 6.2: "We say that M and N are call-by-name equivalent, in symbols M =n N, if M̄ =βη N̄ [CPS images]. More generally, if T is a theory of the λR×+-calculus, we define the call-by-name λµ-theory generated by T to be the set of equations {E | Ē ∈ T}." (bars = CPS translation; rendered without bars by pdftotext). => cbn equality is DEFINED as equality of CPS images, so the CPS translation is faithful on equality BY DEFINITION; the content lies in the axiomatization.
- Prop 6.5: "The theories induced on the λµ-calculus by the call-by-name categorical interpretation are precisely the theories induced by the call-by-name CPS translation." Prop 7.6: same for call-by-value (interpretation in P^op).
- Theorem 3.18 (Structure Theorem): "Any control category P is equivalent to a category of continuations R^C."
- Theorem 6.12: "Let T be a set of equations of the disjunctive λµ-calculus over some fixed signature. Then T is a call-by-name theory if and only if it is a congruence relation on terms that satisfies the equations in Table 6." (Table 6: β→, η→, β∧, η∧, η⊤, ζ→, ζ∧, ... ). Thm 7.16: analogous for call-by-value. Proof of completeness described as "a long and tedious verification" (not mechanised).
- Def 6.2 remark: "the class of call-by-name λµ-theories ... has a finite equational axiomatization. For the fragment without products and disjunction, this follows from Hofmann and Streicher's result (Hofmann and Streicher 1997)." [cites the 1997 LICS version of H&S].
- Lemma 6.10: internal language (Σ,T) of P satisfies P^n_{Σ,T} ≃ P.
- Prop 8.1 (duality): "Both translations preserve CPS transforms, and thus the categorical semantics, up to natural isomorphism of types. It follows that the two translations are mutually inverse, in the sense that M =n µα.L⟨|M|⟩αM∗ and M =v µα.⟨|LMM α|⟩∗, up to natural isomorphisms of types." Following: "the translations do not just preserve equational theories, but in fact, the operational semantics as well. In particular, given appropriate notions of observation, they also preserve observational equivalence."
- Intro: "in the presence of product and disjunction types, the call-by-name and call-by-value λµ-calculi are isomorphic to each other, in the sense that there exist syntactic translations between them that preserve the operational semantics and that are mutually inverse up to isomorphism of types."
### Preserved/reflected (INFERENCE from the above)
- CPS: preserves AND reflects the cbn (resp. cbv) λµ equational theory — reflection is definitional (Def 6.2); Theorem 6.12 shows this equals a finitely axiomatized congruence (Table 6), so CPS is equationally sound and complete for those axioms.
- Duality cbn<->cbv: preserves and reflects equations, but only "up to natural isomorphism of types" and requires product AND disjunction types (and, for the optimized translation, control constants K' in the signature). Hypothesis: the specific disjunctive λµ-calculus; Remark 8.2 notes cbv tensor is premonoidal not monoidal.
### Relevance (INFERENCE)
- F04: positive example where a cross-paradigm translation is faithful on a declared proof-identity congruence — but only because the target identity (βη on CPS images) is CHOSEN to make it so; classical proof identity is not canonical (cbn vs cbv give different theories; Remark 8.2 / premonoidality). Any "declared congruence" must fix the evaluation discipline.

## S4c-1. Maraist, Odersky, Turner, Wadler, "Call-by-name, call-by-value, call-by-need, and the linear lambda calculus"

### Statements (verbatim)
- Prop 3.3 (cbn, Girard A→B ↦ !A ⊸ B): "The translation ° from λname to λlin preserves substitution, types, and reductions: (i) (M[x:=N])° ≡ M°[x:=N°]. (ii) ⊢ M : A if and only if ⊢ M° : A°. (iii) M →→name N if and only if M° →→lin N°."
- Prop 4.3 (cbv, A→B ↦ !(A ⊸ B)): "preserves substitution of values, preserves types, and preserves reductions ... (iii) M →→val N if and only if M* →→lin N*." Backward direction via the call-by-let calculus λlet (conservative over λval for reduction, Prop 5.4).
- Prop 6.5: call-by-need → affine calculus: same iff for reductions.

- Sec 8.3 (EQUALITY) verbatim: "The call-by-name translation is complete for equality. ... The other translations are not complete for equality. For instance, the λval terms (M N) and ((λz. z N) M) are not equal in λval, but their translations are equal in λlin. The same example adopts to λlet and λneed."
- Further: "Plotkin showed that the continuation passing style (CPS) translation from λval into itself is sound but not complete [25]. Moggi designed λcomp to be sound and complete for the monad translation ... and Sabry and Felleisen verified that the CPS translation from λcomp into λval is both sound and complete [31]" (secondary claims about Plotkin/Moggi/Sabry–Felleisen; NOT verified in their originals).
### Preserved / reflected (summary)
- Girard cbn (!A⊸B): derivability/typing preserved+reflected; β-reduction preserved+reflected; β-EQUALITY preserved+reflected (complete). η NOT preserved.
- Girard cbv (!(A⊸B)): typing preserved+reflected; β_v-reduction preserved+reflected; EQUALITY preserved but NOT reflected (explicit counterexample (M N) vs ((λz.zN) M)). η_v not preserved.
### Relevance (INFERENCE)
- F04 (strong): The standard Girard embedding of intuitionistic into linear logic is faithful for derivability but, in its cbv form, NOT faithful on proof identity (identifies distinct λval-terms), and neither form preserves η. Completeness on identity is "fragile" under small changes of translation (author's words). Any R3 "preserve AND reflect identity" requirement fails for this textbook cross-foundation translation unless the source congruence is enlarged (e.g., to Moggi's λc) — evidence that proof identity is translation-relative.

### Mechanism and relevance (INFERENCE)
- The simulation is trivial by design: an EF+Refl_P proof of φ = the P-proof π (as an assignment to the "proof" variables of Refl_P) plus a short EF derivation (evaluating Prf_P on π, which EF does in poly size because Prf_P is p-time). I.e., the P-proof-checker is placed INSIDE an axiom scheme. This is the canonical formal instance of threat F02/F03 ("proof-checker-in-an-axiom"): ANY Cook–Reckhow system becomes "Frege-like" with polynomial overhead once its soundness is added as trusted axioms. It preserves derivability (with p-size), but nothing about proof structure, identity, or locality. Any proposed fixed basis + environment must exclude reflection schemata in the environment, or the universality claim becomes this theorem.

## S3. Harper, Sannella, Tarlecki, "Structured theory presentations and logic representations"
- Bib: Annals of Pure and Applied Logic 67 (1994) (per task prompt; NOT printed on retrieved version). Retrieved version header: "Draft: 10th December 1992"; footnote: "This is an expanded and revised version of the paper 'Structure and Representation in LF' which appeared in Proc. 4th IEEE Symp. on Logic in Computer Science, Asilomar, California, June 1989, pp. 226–237."
- URL retrieved: https://homepages.inf.ed.ac.uk/dts/pub/apal.pdf (Sannella's homepage; DRAFT/PREPRINT, 38 pp.). Page numbers = draft. Status: VERIFIED_SOURCE (draft version; differences from published APAL text unknown).
### Definitions (verbatim)
- Def 2.1: "A consequence relation (CR) is a pair (S, ⊢) where S is a set of sentences and ⊢ ⊆ Fin(S) × S is a binary relation such that 1. (Reflexivity) φ ⊢ φ. 2. (Transitivity) If Γ ⊢ φ and Γ, φ ⊢ ψ, then Γ ⊢ ψ [approx.; symbols lost]. 3. (Weakening) If Γ ⊢ ψ, then Γ, φ ⊢ ψ." (=> only monotone, compact, single-conclusion consequence relations: substructural logics without weakening are OUTSIDE this notion of "logic" — INFERENCE.)
- Def 2.8: "A morphism of consequence relations (CR morphism) s : (S1, ⊢1) → (S2, ⊢2) is a function s : S1 → S2 (the translation of sentences) such that if Γ ⊢1 φ, then sΓ ⊢2 sφ. The CR morphism s is ... conservative if Γ ⊢1 φ whenever sΓ ⊢2 sφ."
- Def 2.10: "A logical system, or logic, is a functor L : Sig_L → CR." Footnote: "Of course this definition captures only some aspects of what is usually meant by the informal notion of 'logical system.'" Contrast with institutions: "institutions present a model-theoretic view of logical systems while the formulation in this paper is centered directly around the notion of a consequence relation."
- Def 5.1: "A morphism of logics ρ : L → L′ is a pair (ρ^Sig, ρ^CR) where ρ^Sig : Sig_L → Sig_L′ is a functor and ρ^CR : L → ρ^Sig ; L′ is a natural transformation."
- Def 5.3: "A logic morphism ρ : L → L′ is a representation iff ρ^Sig is an embedding and each ρ^CR_Σ is conservative. A representation is surjective iff each ρ^CR_Σ is surjective as a function on the underlying sets." Gloss: "the requirement of conservativity implies that each consequence relation of the source is fully and faithfully encoded in the target. Thus if ρ : L → L′ is a logic representation then Γ ⊢L φ iff ρ(Γ) ⊢L′ ρ(φ)."
- Def 6.3: LF(Σ) is "the set of closed LF types over the signature Σ, with the consequence relation induced by type inhabitation": A1,…,An ⊢ A iff x1:A1,…,xn:An ⊢Σ M : A for some M.
- Def 6.10/6.11/6.13: "A logic presentation is a pair (Σ, J) where Σ is an LF signature and J is a finite set of internal type families of Σ." "A logical system is uniformly encodable (in LF) iff there exists a logic presentation (ΣL, JL) and a surjective representation ρL : L → P(ΣL, JL)." "The word 'uniform' reflects the fact that we require a 'natural' encoding of the entire family of consequence relations of L in LF, rather than a signature-by-signature encoding as is suggested by the account in [HHP93]."
- Sec 6: "detailed proofs are omitted."
- Intro: "a necessary condition for the correctness of an encoding of an object logic L in LF is that the consequence relation ⊢L of L be fully and faithfully embedded in the consequence relation ⊢LF of LF by an encoding of the syntax of L as LF terms."
### What is preserved
- ONLY consequence (derivability): preserved (morphism) and reflected (conservative), uniformly in signatures (naturality). PROOFS are existentially quantified away (LF consequence = inhabitation "for some M"); no proof identity, no proof-term bijection in the definition (the HHP93 adequacy bijection of proofs is mentioned informally in Sec. 6 ("terms of type true(⌜φ⌝) ... represent proofs") but is not part of Def 5.3/6.13).
- Negative result in paper: lifting structured presentations along representations: "Structured presentations may not be simply translated via the representation and used in the target logic." Counterexample involving `derive` (hiding); "if an L-presentation P does not involve derive, then Th_L′(ρ̃(P)) = ρ(Th_L(P))". Surjectivity needed for Cor 5.16, but "requiring surjectivity is a rather strong restriction".
### Relevance (INFERENCE)
- F01: HST give the abstract, logic-independent definition of "LF represents logic L" — derivability-level only (R1), uniform in signatures. A ProofBasis R1 claim reduces to this notion; R3 (identity) is not covered.
- F03: In LF, all inference rules of the object logic are constants of the signature Σ (the "environment"); the definition places NO restriction on what Σ may contain beyond LF typing, so trusted rules live entirely in the signature.
- Scope limit: CRs require weakening (Def 2.1), so linear/relevant logics are not "logics" in this sense.

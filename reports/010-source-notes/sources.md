## 6. Kelly & Lack, On property-like structures — VERIFIED_SOURCE
URL http://www.tac.mta.ca/tac/volumes/1997/n9/n9.pdf ; TAC Vol. 3, No. 9, 1997, pp. 213–250.
- Abstract p.213: "A category may bear many monoidal structures, but (to within a unique isomorphism) only one structure of "category with finite products". ... we consider on a 2-category those 2-monads for which algebra structure is essentially unique if it exists ... We call such 2-monads property-like."
- p.216 (Intro): "we call T property-like when T-algebra structure is essentially unique in this stronger sense; which is equally to say that T has both essential uniqueness of algebra structure (in the original sense) and uniqueness of morphism structure. Thus the 2-monad for categories with finite coproducts is property-like; and so dually is that for categories with finite products."
- Formal definition: §3 p.~228 (twelve conditions (XYZ)); "we took the conjunction (IEM) ∧ (AUM) as our definition of "property-like"" ; §4 p.229 restates; Theorem 4.2 gives equivalents. Def 5.3 (fully property-like) p.234; Prop 6.1 (lax-/colax-idempotent ⇒ property-like) p.235.
- Examples found: finite coproducts (lax-idempotent), finite products (colax-idempotent), distributive categories (property-like, neither). Monoidal structure = NOT property-like (counterexample in intro). Also semigroup-related monad on Sgrp' (Prop 5.2) property-like without (2M).
- NOT FOUND in text: "cartesian closed" as an example (grep for "closed" finds only "monoidal closed" in intro p.213-214 as an example of structure not given by a 2-monad on Cat). Do NOT cite K&L for CCC being property-like.
- Intro p.213-214: "monoidal closed categories, for instance, are the algebras for a 2-monad on the 2-category Cat_g of categories, functors, and natural isomorphisms, but not for any 2-monad on Cat".

## 4. Lawvere, Functorial Semantics of Algebraic Theories — VERIFIED_SOURCE
URL http://www.tac.mta.ca/tac/reprints/articles/5/tr5.pdf (Reprints in TAC No. 5; printed page = PDF page)
- Author's comments p.8 item (5): "it is more accurate to apply the term "theory", not to the presentations as had become traditional in formalist logic, but rather to the more invariant abstract concepts themselves which serve a pivotal role, both in their connection with the syntax of presentations, as well as with the semantics of representations."
- p.19: original thesis uses coproduct convention: "an algebraic theory is a category with coproducts rather than with products" (now "anachronistic").
- Ch. II §1 p.62 Definition: T is the full subcategory of ({S0},C1) of objects S0 → A such that A commutes with finite coproducts and |A| is an isomorphism (bijective on objects).
- Ch. II §2 p.71 Definition: "By a presentation of an algebraic theory is meant a triple S,E,f ... The theory presented by S,E,f is the coequalizer in T of the maps EF ⇉ SF". Thm II.2.1 p.69: free algebraic theory exists over each sequence of sets.
- ADVERSARIAL: Remark p.65: groups 'presented' with single binary x·y^{-1} vs (e, ^{-1}, ·): "This actually gives two theories, for in the first case no constant is expressible and in the second case the (only) constant e is expressible. ... according to the first theory the empty set is a group, whereas to the second it is not." -> presentations that look equivalent need not give isomorphic Lawvere theories (nullary/empty-model issue).
- Remark p.89: an algebraic category "may be represented ... for many different non-isomorphic theories" (Morita-type non-uniqueness of theory given category of models).
- No explicit theorem "theory independent of presentation"; it is by construction (the presented theory is the coequalizer; two presentations present the 'same' theory iff the coequalizers are isomorphic) — our reading, not a quoted statement.
- 1968 article p.109: product-convention summary: "An algebraic theory is a category A having as objects 1, A, A^2, ... and ... morphisms Π_i^(n): A^n → A ... such that for any n morphisms θ_i: A^m → A there is exactly one morphism <θ0..θn−1>: A^m → A^n ..."

## 9. Derivable vs admissible — VERIFIED_SOURCE (informal sources; Iemhoff's major survey NOT_ACCESSED)
(a) Course note "ADMISSIBLE RULES" (UvA Proof Theory 2014, B. van den Berg course page; author not named in file), https://staff.fnwi.uva.nl/b.vandenberg3/Onderwijs/Proof_Theory_2014/admissible%20rules.pdf , §2 Def 1, p.1: "A rule ϕ/ψ is said to be admissible if for all substitutions σ, if ⊢ σϕ, then ⊢ σψ. A rule ϕ/ψ is said to be derivable if ⊢ ϕ → ψ. Clearly, every derivable rule is admissible. We shall see that, in general, not every admissible rule is derivable." Def 2: structurally complete = every admissible rule derivable. §1 examples: cut for IPC/CPC; □A/A for K; Con(PA)/⊥ for PA.
(b) Iemhoff, "The rules of intermediate logic" (de Jongh Festschrift), https://festschriften.illc.uva.nl/D65/iemhoff.pdf , p.1: "A rule A/B is admissible for a theory if B is provable in it whenever A is. The rule A/B is said to be derivable if the theory proves that A → B. Classical propositional logic CPC does not have any non-derivable admissible rules ... intuitionistic propositional logic IPC has many admissible rules that are not derivable", e.g. IPR ¬A → B∨C / (¬A→B)∨(¬A→C).
CAVEAT: both define derivability via an implication connective (uses deduction theorem); for general deductive systems "derivable" = conclusion derivable from premises as hypotheses — that general formulation is UNVERIFIED-MEMORY here. Admissibility is not preserved under extensions (per search summary of Iemhoff Tbilisi slides — SECONDARY/not downloaded).

## 10. Internal category and externalisation — VERIFIED_SOURCE (Streicher notes)

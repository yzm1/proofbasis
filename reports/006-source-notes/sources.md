## 1. Novikov–Boone (unsolvable word problem)
- **SECONDARY_ONLY.** Source: Bridson & Nyberg-Brodda, "HNN extensions and embedding theorems for groups", arXiv:2512.10800v1 (11 Dec 2025), §3.1, around p. 10. Quote: "P. S. Novikov [Nov55] and W. Boone [Boo57] had, in 1955 and 1957, respectively, published their independent proofs of the general undecidability of the word problem for groups." The same section also says: "the group specified in Novikov's 1952 announcement paper [Nov52] may actually have had decidable word problem" (footnote 2).
- The same paper (§3.2, p. 11) derives a f.p. group with undecidable WP from Higman's theorem. It uses the relators b^{-f(n)} a b^{f(n)} = d^{-f(n)} c d^{f(n)}, where f is recursive with non-recursive range. Then "b^{-m} a b^{m} = d^{-m} c d^{m} if and only if m lies in the range of f".
- **Σ1-completeness / r.e. degree:** SECONDARY_ONLY. Source: arXiv:2512.10800 §3.3, p. 12: "Higman's Embedding Theorem can be shown to be made to preserve the Turing degree of the word problem [Cla64, Val75]." [Cla64] is C. R. J. Clapham, "Finitely presented groups with word problems of arbitrary degrees of insolubility", Proc. London Math. Soc. (1964). The step "therefore some f.p. group has an r.e.-complete WP" is my inference: take f with range K and use the Higman construction above. It is an informal argument and is not a quoted statement.
- KMS arXiv:1204.6506v5, §1.2 (VERIFIED_SOURCE for their text): "WPyes ... The algorithm Ayes enumerates one by one all consequences of the defining relations R". This means the yes-part of the WP of any f.p. structure is r.e.

## 2. Higman embedding theorem; universal f.p. group
- **SECONDARY_ONLY (verbatim restatements, with the primary locus given).**
  - Mikaelian, arXiv:1908.10153v8, §1: "Theorem 1.1 (Theorem 1 in [10]). A finitely generated group can be embedded in a finitely presented group if and only if it is recursively presented." Here [10] = Higman, Proc. R. Soc. A 262 (1961) 455–475.
  - Recursive presentation is defined there as "the set of defining relations {r1, r2, ...} is recursive enumerable".
  - Bridson–Nyberg-Brodda, arXiv:2512.10800 §3.2, p. 11: "Higman's Embedding Theorem ([Hig61, Theorem 1, p. 456]). A finitely generated group can be embedded in a finitely presented group if and only if it is recursively presented." The same section has "Corollary 3.1. Any recursively presented group can be effectively embedded in a finitely presented group." This covers countably generated groups, via HNN49 Theorem II, and they attribute it to Higman's introduction.
- **Universal group:** arXiv:2512.10800 §3.3, p. 12: "we have just shown that there exists a finitely presented group containing an isomorphic copy of every finitely presented group. Such groups are often called universal (finitely presented) groups."
  - Explicit universal groups are attributed there to Boone–Collins 1974 (26 relations) and Valiev 1977 (21 relations).
  - Wikipedia (pointer only) adds that the f.g. subgroups of such a group are exactly the f.g. recursively presented groups.
- Bergstra–Tucker IW 115/79, Thm 2.4 (VERIFIED_SOURCE, scanned OCR, p. 12): "There exists a group G finitely generated by g1,...,gn which is semicomputable, but not computable, and contains (an isomorphic copy of) every semicomputable group, such that (G,g1,...,gn) has a (F,V,N) specification." The proof says "By Higman's Theorem [3] ... contain a copy of every finitely generated semicomputable group". The proof proves the finitely generated version.

## 3. McKinsey / Mal'cev / Dyson / Mostowski (f.p. residually finite implies solvable WP)
- **SECONDARY_ONLY.**
  - Rauzy, arXiv:2002.02540v2, Introduction: "a theorem of McKinsey ([9, 11, 3]) states that all finitely presented residually finite groups have solvable word problem".
    - [9] McKinsey, JSL 8(3) (1943) 61–76.
    - [11] Mostowski, Fund. Math. 59 (1966).
    - [3] Dyson, Notices AMS 1964.
  - KMS arXiv:1204.6506v5 §1.2 states the algorithm for a "residually finite finitely presented algebraic structure of finite type (signature) T (say, groups, semigroups, rings, etc.)". It runs Ayes (enumerate consequences) in parallel with Ano: "enumerates all homomorphisms φ1, φ2, ... of G into finite algebraic structures of type T and waits until φi(w) ≠ φi(w′)."
  - Hypotheses: finite presentation plus finite signature.
  - Counterexamples when only recursively presented: Meskin 1974 and Dyson 1974 give f.g. recursively presented RF groups with unsolvable WP (as cited by Rauzy and KMS).
  - KMS main theorem: for any recursive f there is an f.p. RF solvable group in which both the yes and no parts take time at least f(n).
  - The attribution to Mal'cev is UNVERIFIED-MEMORY. None of the retrieved texts mentions Mal'cev.

## 4. Mazurkiewicz traces
- **VERIFIED_SOURCE (primary, 1977).** Source: A. Mazurkiewicz, "Concurrent Program Schemes and their Interpretations", DAIMI PB-78 (Aarhus, July 1977). doi:10.7146/dpb.v6i78.7691, from https://tidsskrift.dk/daimipb/article/download/7691/6542. It is a scan; I OCR'd it and checked against the page image (report pp. 5–7).
  - Definition: "Define ≐ ... as the least equivalence relation in V* satisfying ... (a,b) is in I => w'abw" ≐ w'baw"" (1.1). Here independence means "a ≠ b and either (a,b) is in I or (b,a) is in I".
  - Traces are "the equivalence classes of ≐ relation". The text adds: "This abstraction converts then sequences of symbols, i.e. linearly ordered sets of symbol occurrences, into partially ordered sets of such occurrences".
  - Theorem 1.1, verbatim: "Any partial ordering relation Q in a set X is uniqely determined by a nonempty family of linear orderings in X (namely, the family of all extensions of Q to linear orderings); conversely, any nonempty family of linear orderings in X determines uniquely a partial ordering in X (namely, the intersection of all members of the family)." Proof is via Szpilrajn–Marczewski.
  - Caveat: this report does **not** state, as a theorem, the bijection between traces and dependence graphs or labelled posets. It only gives this order-theoretic lemma plus Example 1.1 and its graph. It also shows that ≐ is a congruence: [w][u]=[wu] is well defined.
- **SECONDARY_ONLY:** Lê, arXiv:1011.1030v4, §1.1, describes "three equivalent methods": congruence classes, dependence graphs (citing [8, Chapter 2] = Book of Traces), and labelled posets. It says these "can be shown to be equivalent" but gives no theorem there.

## 6. Chaudhuri–Miller–Saurin, "Canonical sequent proofs via multi-focusing", IFIP TCS 2008
- **VERIFIED_SOURCE (author-hosted version).** Source: https://www.lix.polytechnique.fr/~dale/papers/tcs08trackb.pdf (15 pp.). I did not compare it with the published Springer version (pp. 383–396, doi 10.1007/978-0-387-09680-3_26), so theorem numbering may differ.
  - Setting: cut-free one-sided MALL with units.
  - "Definition 1 Two proofs D and E ⊢ Γ are iso-initial, written D ≃ E, if each can be rewritten to the other using local permutations and the set of initial sequents in both D and E are the same."
  - "Definition 4 ... iso-polar, written D ≈ D′, if they are equal up to permutations restricted to the pos/pos and neg/neg types."
  - "Definition 6 A proof D of ⊢ Γ m ∆ is maximal if for every sub-proof E ⊢ Γ′ ⇑ · of D, it is the case for any E′ ≃ E ⊢ Γ′ ⇑ · that roots(E′) ⊆ roots(E)."
  - "Theorem 7 (canonicity) If D ≃ E ⊢ Γ ⇑ · are both maximal, then D ≈ E."
  - "Theorem 16 Two maximally multi-focused MLL− proofs of ⊢ · ⇑ Γ are iso-polar iff they have the same MLL− proof net."
  - Caveats:
    - Canonicity is only up to ≈, not syntactic uniqueness.
    - ≃ deliberately excludes permutations that change the initial sequents (⊤ issue). The paper says it is "decidedly not equating all proofs that are equated in the standard categorical model of MALL".
    - The proof-net bijection covers only unit-free MLL. "The problem of proof-nets for MALL with units is still open" (as of 2008).

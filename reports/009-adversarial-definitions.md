## 1. Executive verdict

**Neither of the two candidate non-vacuity criteria survives.** They are:

1. **N_split:** an effective, composition/substitution/binding-preserving retraction from all target proofs at translated interfaces to source proofs.
2. **N_rep:** a faithful categorical semantics through representable presheaves, with proof operations acting naturally on all probes.

An explicit serialized proof-checker representation and a typed-path rule-table representation satisfy **both**. They even admit two-sided inverses on proof classes. Thus adding relative fullness would not repair these particular false positives. Yoneda explains why N_rep adds no discriminator to an already faithful compositional interface. N_split adds a real restriction, but the restriction concerns retracts rather than the distinction between mathematical reasoning and interpretation.

There is also an exact obstruction to N_split on the universal-group control: **no fixed finitely presented group U admits retractions onto every finite cyclic group C_p**. Abelianization proves this, even though a universal U embeds every C_p. This excludes every embedding into that fixed group-word interface for a suitable p, not every possible proof algebra or compound interface.

The strongest resulting limitation is precise: **an invariant depending only on the structured proof-class interface cannot distinguish two presentations with isomorphic such interfaces.** The checker and path constructions below supply the isomorphism, including composition and their one-port discipline. This is a bounded indistinguishability result, not a theorem that every imaginable non-vacuity invariant fails.

**Recommendation: DEFINITION UNRESOLVED; NARROW to one additional definition decision, not to a smaller source class.** Retain the reconciliation's universal objective. Do not begin its existence theorem. Specify what structure, beyond the quotient interface, distinguishes a permitted realization from a checker realization, and what equivalences must preserve that structure.

## 2. An explicit faithful interface

### 2.1 Objects and quantifiers

Retain **C** from Report 002 §2.1 with the reconciliation's finite proof-equation presentations: finite sorted second-order object syntax, finitely many judgment/rule schemas, and the least typed congruence generated by finitely many declared proof equations. Rule application has no unaccounted computational side condition. Conversion or global validity must be represented by explicit judgments/certificates if admitted. This includes binding, multiple judgment forms and resource-sensitive presentations; it is not redefined as a group or path class.

For S, an interface consists of:

- a mathematical environment E with an explicit list of syntax declarations, definitions, axioms and already justified evidence;
- an object-variable context Δ;
- a labeled hypothesis context Γ, with its permitted weakening, contraction, exchange/order and splitting operations;
- a finite list H of typed derivation holes, including hypothetical/parametric holes with their binder scopes;
- a judgment J in these contexts.

Write `D_S(E; Δ; Γ; H ⊢ J)` for its derivations and `P_S = D_S/≈_S`. Holes are not assumed valid closed theorems. Filling them is a partial, typed operation; filling dependent holes must respect their scopes. The presentations supply identity and grafting/substitution laws wherever these are defined. For categorical tests, write `P_S` also for the resulting category of admissible contexts and substitutions; a one-output proof is the corresponding component. If only a multicategorical composition is supplied, retain that structure rather than assume Cartesian products of contexts.

A fixed target A has a finite signature of typed schemas, fixed typing/equality rules and arbitrarily many generated types and proofs. The interface translation comprises effective maps

```text
T_S : expressions and their sorted contexts → target expressions/contexts
L_S : mathematical environments → target environments
B_S : labeled hypothesis/hole boundaries → target boundaries
Q_S : judgments at those boundaries → target judgments
F_S : source derivations → target derivations.
```

**Compound and indexed interfaces are permitted at this baseline.** Atomic source formulas need not map to atomic target types. A context map records each source slot's target type or block, its scope and structural permissions. No finite number of atomic profiles is inferred from a finite signature. A target type containing source-description data is permitted by this interface definition; N, not an unstated exclusion, must decide whether it is scientifically adequate.

### 2.2 Fidelity obligations

For **every** well-formed source E, Δ, Γ, H and J, after any admissible closing/filling of holes:

```text
D_S(E; Δ; Γ ⊢ J) ≠ ∅
  iff D_A(L_SE; T_SΔ; B_SΓ ⊢ Q_SJ) ≠ ∅.                 (I1)

d ≈_S e iff F_Sd ≈_A F_Se, for d,e at the same boundary. (I2)

F_S(id) ≈_A id at the interpreted interface;
F_S(d[θ]) ≈_A F_S(d)[T_Sθ, F_Sθ_proof].                 (I3)
```

I3 includes capture-avoiding object substitution, scoped proof substitution and grafting in **derivation** holes, not merely substitution of formulas. On a non-native interface the displayed right side denotes the specified typed target grafting operation; it must be a derived operation of A, with identity/associativity and binder-renaming coherence. Calling arbitrary decoding “substitution” does not establish those obligations. Native LF or λ examples use ordinary substitution. This distinction must be declared before testing a candidate translation.

I1 quantifies over **all** target inhabitants of the indicated judgment, not just images and not those satisfying an additional, undeclared image-membership test. It does not require all those inhabitants to equal source images. I2 compares proofs, not conclusions. I3 requires schematic templates valid with open holes, not a translator that independently recompiles each closed input.

**Resource obligation I4:** B_S exposes its slot/block and modality map. Each translated rule has a typing derivation respecting the stated transport of structural permissions, splitting and order. For genuinely linear slots, a block is used linearly as a block; changing one slot to several wires must be recorded. If an additional usage observation is required on proof **classes**, supply maps u_S,u_A invariant under the selected congruences and require `u_A(Fd)=u_S(d)` under the boundary map. Do not universally require raw occurrence counts: β-erasure already makes those counts non-invariant. This is a proposed precise interface package, not a claim that every member of C admits it.

I1 excludes extra translated judgments. I4 adds the declared port/permission discipline, which I1 alone does not recover. For example, a Cartesian representative `π₁ Δ : p→p` equals identity yet copies and deletes an intermediate occurrence. A policy concerning raw representatives and a policy concerning observable classes give different answers. Neither should be silently substituted for the other.

### 2.3 Trust and equality

Require a **provenance map**: each trusted target assumption corresponds to an actual source assumption, with its scope and resource mode. Each imported theorem carries a derivation from the fixed core and mapped dependencies. Extra declarations may introduce object syntax or transparent conservative abbreviations; they may not introduce trusted proof constructors or source-specific primitive proof equations. For a source rule r, the translation must exhibit an A-derivation with the translated premise holes open. Having a derivation *from a new assumed rule constant* does not meet this condition.

This does not ban mathematical axioms merely because they are schematic. ZF or a choice principle already listed among a source's assumptions is different in provenance from a checker introduced only to validate the encoding. Classification is relative to the supplied source environment. There is no intrinsic “nonlogical type” test in a propositions-as-types language without an independently fixed sort/judgment discipline.

Keep fixed target congruence `≈_A` distinct from inhabitation of an internal equality proposition. An internal equality certificate is a proof of another judgment; it is not automatically the equality used in I2. If a foundation has equality reflection, the contextual equality judgment must be indexed by the mapped environment and have the same fixed reflection rule. Then “assumptions cannot affect any equality” would exclude that foundation; “no new primitive equation schemas” need not. This is an explicit alternative interface choice, not a way to import uncertified relations.

The existential target statement remains

```text
∃ fixed finite A  ∀ S∈C  ∃ effective (T_S,L_S,B_S,Q_S,F_S)
  ∀ source interfaces: I1–I4 and the provenance obligations.
```

Each F is effective; uniform discovery of F from S is a different requirement. Equality need not be decidable, and no efficiency claim is made. These quantifiers retain the broad ambition; the five controls are tests, not a replacement C.

### 2.4 Explicit ambiguity and incompatibility controls

**INFERENCE — rule versus assumption.** In implicational natural deduction with βη, fix p and let `K=¬¬p→p`. Extend the calculus by a primitive unary constructor `r(f):p` for `f:¬¬p`, with no r computation equation. Compare ordinary deduction under `k:K`. The maps

```text
r(f) ↦ k f;             k ↦ λz.r(z)
```

are inverse modulo βη, commute with binding/substitution, and preserve/reflect proof classes and derivability at corresponding contexts. However, adding k when the primitive-rule source's E is empty fails the provenance condition above. A two-world Kripke model with p true only at the later world forces ¬¬p but not p at the earlier world, so K was not derived from the intuitionistic core. Equivalent proof presentations need not have equivalent **trust annotations**. Requiring invariance under this change while also forbidding it by provenance is inconsistent; invariance must specify whether it preserves the ledger.

**INFERENCE — resource interface.** In unit-free intuitionistic multiplicative linear logic set `w(p)=1`, `w(A⊗B)=w(A)+w(B)` and `w(A⊸B)=w(B)−w(A)`. Rule induction gives `w(Γ)=w(B)` for every proof of Γ⊢B. Thus `p⊢p⊗p` is impossible. Translating tensor as Cartesian product with unrestricted p admits `(x,x)`, violating I1. This defeats that direct representation, not translations using ! or typed resource indices.

**INFERENCE — causal interface.** `(λz.x)((λu.u)y)≈β x` erases raw events; a finite dependency cycle cannot map injectively, preserving every edge, into a strict poset. Unfolded cyclic occurrences can instead have an infinite ancestor poset. Conflict between alternative uses of one token is also invisible inside either single-event trace. Accordingly H_causal requires a separately supplied, congruence-compatible event/configuration structure. No universal occurrence-poset is appended to I1–I4 here. Global cyclic validity requires its own certificate and composition theorem; a decidable check does not establish either certificate irrelevance or compositionality. Brotherston's precise decidability result is recorded in §8.

## 3. Two rival criteria — definitions frozen before their tests

These are independently familiar mathematical requirements, not definitions claimed to solve fundamentality. Test **these exact predicates**, rather than strengthening one after an attack.

### N_split: effective structural retract

Let `B_F` be the target proof structure restricted to translated source boundaries, with **all** target proof classes at those boundaries. It retains the interface's admitted substitutions, binder operations and resource annotations. Require an effective map G on its derivations, respecting target equality, such that:

```text
G : B_F → P_S;
G(id)=id; G(g∘f)=G(g)∘G(f);
G commutes with the specified typed substitutions, grafting and binders;
G F = id_P_S, modulo source equality.
```

The object/context map returns a translated boundary to its specified source boundary. This is a split monomorphism in the category of the specified proof structures. G need not itself be fundamental or a core macro. **Do not require `FG=id_B_F`: N_split does not add fullness.**

Motivation: recover source construction from a target proof, with compositional rather than theorem-by-theorem reconstruction. Extra target proofs may remain. This is stronger than I1–I3 and is stable under effective isomorphisms of the structured interfaces. It might appear to rule out opaque interpreter executions. It does not.

### N_rep: faithful representable semantics

For the compositional context category `B_F`, require a presheaf category `[B_F^op,Set]` and a fully faithful realization Y of its objects/arrows by **representables**, such that `YF` is faithful and its arrows are natural transformations preserving identities/composition. The source interface's other structure is still governed by I3–I4; N_rep does not invent additional binder laws.

In equations, for each b let `Yb(c)=B_F(c,b)`, and require

```text
B_F(b,b′) ≅ Nat(Yb,Yb′),
(Yf)_c(h)=f∘h,
Y(g∘f)=Yg∘Yf,
d≈_S e iff YF(d)=YF(e).
```

The semantic parameter c ranges over **all probes**, not a chosen set of test executions. There is no computability or finite-model assumption on these hom-sets. Motivation: proof identity and composition acquire a faithful, representation-independent mathematical semantics in terms of universal elements/natural transformations.

**VERIFIED_SOURCE:** Yoneda, as stated and proved in Leinster Theorem 4.2.1, implies the representable hom-bijection. Consequently **N_rep is automatic for every small faithful compositional proof-category interface**. The proof in §4 demonstrates the defect, rather than merely asserting that an encoding “looks computational.” A stronger atom-parametricity condition is a different predicate; §6 gives a further attack on that idea.

## 4. Required test 1 — explicit proof-checker interpretation

**INFERENCE — complete bounded representation.** Use the independently given family of finite directed multigraphs G, with distinct edge identifiers. The source calculus S_G has finitely many fixed vertex colors, identity, one unary rule for each edge `a:u→v`, and typed cut/composition. The colors can be specified as finitely many judgment forms; there are no object-parameter variables or binders in this fixture. Proof equality has only associativity/unit equations. Thus proof classes are finite edge paths. There are no closed proofs or arrows between disconnected vertices.

Here is an actual checker, whose input is a serialized derivation, not a purported native inference:

```text
verify(G, Id(v))         = (v,v, [])
verify(G, Edge(a,t))     = let (u,v,w)=verify(G,t);
                          require lookup(G,a)=(v,z);
                          return (u,z,w·[a])
verify(G, Cut(t,t′))     = let (u,v,w)=verify(G,t);
                          let (v′,z,w′)=verify(G,t′);
                          require v=v′;
                          return (u,z,w·w′)
```

Malformed syntax or a failed test rejects. The recursion is on the finite tree. Table equality and lookup are ordinary finite-list algorithms. “Require” describes this total validator; in the target presentation it is an explicit equality/lookup certificate, **not** a computational side condition granting a proof.

Define a fixed target A_check with natural-number/list/tree data, relational lookup, list append, and a judgment `Ck(G,t,u,v,w)`. Its finite checker rules are exactly the three displayed cases with their returned tuples: the Edge case has both a child Ck premise and a lookup premise; Cut has two child premises sharing the middle vertex. Equality comparisons are expressed by shared indices or explicit equality derivations. All these are fixed schemas over data G, not one trusted constructor per source edge. Invalid tables/identifiers have no lookup derivation.

The interpreted one-port proof judgment is

```text
Hom_check((G,u),(G,v)) := packages box(w,t,c),
                         c : Ck(G,t,u,v,w).
```

All checking evidence is administrative. A **fixed**, typed equation schema identifies `box(w,t,c)≈box(w,t′,c′)` at a common G,u,v,w. Append has its fixed recursion and monoid equations. Identity is the empty-word box. There is a fixed typed proof operation `comp(p,q)` with unit/associativity equations and

```text
comp(box(w,t,c),box(w′,t′,c′))
  ≈ box(w·w′,Cut(t,t′),ckCut(c,c′)).
```

**Proofs are an opaque sort:** the only operations producing a Hom proof are box, identity and comp. There is no Hom→data projection and no general eliminator that scrutinizes an open Hom proof. Data and checking-certificate operations cannot take Hom proofs as inputs. This finite sorted presentation is not an ambient type theory with unrestricted datatype elimination. Thus an open source proof hole translates to an open target proof hole, not to its nonexistent serialized data projection. No source proof equation is added to A_check. The objects carry the source graph **as data in the translated interface**, not as assumed proof generators.

The zero-input source boundary maps to a separate `Closed(G,v)` judgment with no constructors. A package at Hom((G,u),(G,v)) is a **one-input arrow**, even when its data representation is a closed metatheoretic term; it is not a proof at the zero-input boundary. This distinction is needed for I1 and for the port claim. This fixture tests one-port composition, not a claim that an unrestricted data variable natively represents every linear hypothesis.

Induction on a Ck derivation shows that w is a path from u to v. Conversely, a path constructs an accepted nested Edge tree starting at Id(u). Induction on source terms shows their checker outputs are exactly their flattened paths. Associativity/unit preserve flattening; eliminating Id/Cut gives the nested-edge representative for that path. Conversely, interpretation into the free path category distinguishes different words. Hence

```text
P_S_G(u,v) ≅ Hom_check((G,u),(G,v))/≈_check ≅ Paths_G(u,v).
```

Define F homomorphically: identity maps to its box; an edge rule maps to composition with its singleton-edge box; cut maps to comp; a proof hole maps to that hole. The singleton box has a finite lookup witness for its literal edge code. Every **closed** F(d) is equal to the box of d's serialized tree, returned path and checking certificate, by the comp equation and box irrelevance. Thus source proofs really have checker-accepted serialized representatives, while open holes are handled schematically.

Define G on proof terms: a variable maps to its source hole, comp to source composition, and a box to the source path decoded from its word. In these translated contexts all data arguments are closed: there are only Hom proof holes and no proof-to-data operations. Finite data computations normalize to literal lists; a Ck derivation ensures their words are valid paths. G respects every displayed proof equation. Structural induction gives grafting naturality; GF and FG are identities on proof classes, including open proof-hole contexts. Both maps are effective. Empty hom-sets and the single input/output ports agree. Binding is absent and therefore imposes no extra law in this fixture. E is empty and remains empty.

**Result:** N_split **YES**, N_rep **YES**. This is an explicit checker false positive under the intended prohibition. It is stronger than encoding only theorem truth: proof classes and composition really survive. The core is a finite declarative checker; calling its constructors “derived proof operations” does not change how the source rules enter through G and the serialized tree.

**Scope:** this is a checker for the entire graph-rule family, not a construction for every S∈C or a universal existence proof. A universal machine can execute the displayed validator, but no all-C binding/equality compiler theorem is inferred from that observation. Rejecting only a checker capable of running *every* program while admitting this explicit rule-table checker would leave the reconciliation's second negative control untouched.

Certificate irrelevance is essential. Without the fixed box equation, different Id/Cut trees with the same path can retain extra checker identities; the claimed two-sided inverse then fails. This explicitly resolves, in this fixture only, Report 007's warning about forgetting validity certificates.

**Self-attack and correction:** a draft allowed word projections from arbitrary open packages and reconstructed only closed proofs. That does not justify N_split. For a one-loop source, inspection of a proof-hole word could return the edge on even lengths and identity on odd lengths; no substitution-natural source word template has that behavior. The opaque-sort presentation above removes that operation, without changing either candidate predicate. The argument therefore applies to this explicit sorted checker, not to arbitrary dependent-record or universal-machine encodings with proof inspection.

## 5. Required test 2 — typed paths interpreting source rule tables

Use exactly the same S_G. The fixed target A_path instead has

```text
nil_u : Path(G,u,u)
cons(a,ℓ,p) : Path(G,u,v)
  where ℓ : Lookup(G,a,u,z), p : Path(G,z,v).
```

The tables have unique edge IDs and canonical lookup witnesses. Quotient any administrative lookup/equality certificates; keep the edge word. Concatenation is specified by its fixed recursion equations on nil/cons, with associativity/unit laws. These are not per-G imports. As in §4, there is no general proof-to-data eliminator: the proof operations are nil, cons and concatenation, while lookup arguments are data. Source identity maps to nil, edges to singleton paths, and composition to concatenation. Every closed target class is a well-typed word; open terms also contain opaque path holes. Structural decoding of cons and concatenation gives the source path template, commutes with grafting and is inverse on classes.

**Result:** N_split **YES**, N_rep **YES**, for the same reasons as test 1. This is simultaneously free-category mathematics and interpretation of an encoded rule table. No term-level criterion is justified merely by calling cons “geometric composition” rather than “execute the next rule.”

**Bounded indistinguishability lemma (INFERENCE).** The effective maps

```text
H : B_check → B_path,       box(w,t,c) ↦ the typed word w;
                           comp and holes ↦ concatenation and holes
K : B_path → B_check,       nil/cons/concat/holes ↦ their checker templates
```

are inverse on classes, preserve the translated objects, empty/nonempty hom-sets, composition, grafting and one-port resource annotations, and satisfy `H F_check=F_path`. If an invariant N depends only on this structured interface diagram and is invariant under such isomorphisms, then

```text
N(A_check,F_check)=N(A_path,F_path).
```

This follows by transporting the diagram along H; it is not an undecidability argument. Both tested criteria obey it. More generally, a native presentation of the free category has the same interface diagram. A predicate that accepts that presentation and rejects this checker must inspect some structure **not retained in the diagram**.

**Limit:** the lemma does not equate the entire target algebras, their unrelated types, raw evaluation events or their generators. An invariant inspecting those could distinguish them. Nor does it prove that every structured invariant fails on arbitrary foundations. It identifies exactly the information a proposed invariant would have to add.

## 6. Required test 3 — universal finitely presented groups

**Published input:** there is a finitely presented group U containing every finitely presented group. Higman's original Theorem 1 states the finitely generated recursively presented embedding criterion. The original article was not retrieved here; that theorem remains **SECONDARY_ONLY** through the exact restatement and universal-group construction in Bridson–Nyberg-Brodda §§3.2–3.3. Independently inspected **primary evidence** is Bergstra–Tucker Theorem 2.4 and its proof: they choose a finitely presented G containing every finitely generated semicomputable group and give its finite variable-equation specification. Their theorem's printed “every semicomputable group” is restricted by the proof to finitely generated groups. No broader reading is needed.

For a finite presentation `G=⟨g_i | R_j⟩`, S_G has unary g_i and inverse rules at each atom p, cancellation and relator equations over a typed proof hole, and no closed or cross-atom proofs. Its endomorphism classes are G: outer rule application and inner grafting supply the two-sided word congruence. All are finite derivations; group relations are not cyclic proof graphs.

The fixed A_U has U's generators/inverses, cancellation and relators as uniform unary schemas at a fresh atom X_p. Select an embedding `i:G↪U` and generator words w_i. Translate g_i to the derived operation w_i, atoms to atoms, and grafting to group multiplication. No rule table, code parameter, assumed inference rule or source-specific target equation is installed. For each G the finitely many selected words give an effective F; effective synthesis of these words is not required by the quantifiers. This verifies the group control in Report 006 independently at the level of the proof-category argument.

**N_rep: YES** for every such G. Yoneda of the one-object group category is faithful. Universality of U alone is not a reason to call this a bad representation: it really preserves the selected one-port proof symmetries and equality. It does not expose additional logical operations which these sources do not have, and it establishes nothing about all C. D2's native-atom, fixed-wrapper and derived-template tests also admit it; that acceptance is not a contradiction.

**N_split: NO as a family-wide criterion for this fixed target**, with an explicit counterexample for every U.

**Retraction obstruction (INFERENCE).** Let U be any finitely generated group, in particular the fixed finitely presented universal group. Its abelianization B is finitely generated abelian, so its torsion subgroup has finite order t. Choose a prime p not dividing t. If any embedding `i:C_p→U` had a homomorphic left inverse `r:U→C_p`, abelianization would give

```text
C_p → B → C_p, with composite id.
```

The first map is injective. But B has no nonzero p-torsion: its free part has none and its finite torsion has order prime to p. Contradiction. For a supplied finite presentation, the abelianization is obtained from the finite relator exponent matrix, so this p can even be selected by Smith normal form and a prime search. No computation was run here.

Take the finite source with unary r and equation `r^p(ξ)≈ξ`. Universality supplies its embedding into U, with equality and derivability reflection. Any N_split inverse at its translated atomic boundary would be precisely the prohibited homomorphism U→C_p. This defeats **every embedding of that source into this word interface**, not just an unfortunate generator word. For some other G, a retraction may exist; the assertion is not that none do.

Thus reversibility excludes genuine faithful mathematical embeddings permitted by H0, without excluding the checker in §4. It could be adopted as a stricter research objective, but it has not become an interpreter filter. Other A with different generated interfaces are not excluded by this obstruction.

**Further parameter attack (INFERENCE).** A stronger informal suggestion is that operations should be natural in arbitrary atom values. For sets X, put `K_D(X)=Set(D,X)`. Every natural transformation `K_D⇒K_D` is precomposition by a unique `h:D→D`: evaluate the transformation at `id_D`, then use naturality with the function `f:D→X`. Conversely precomposition is natural.

Choose D=U and `h_u(d)=du`. The transformations `f↦f∘h_u` give a faithful group action (with the appropriate composition convention), detected at X=D,f=id. They obey the group equations and are natural in **every** map X→Y. This closes the set-level naturality calculation behind Report 006's E8; it is not a claim that an arbitrary parametric type theory has a faithful model or that its full derivability/equality interface is adequate. A finite group-data theory with the fixed U relators expresses these translations. Banning type-case or demanding this naturality alone cannot prevent group words from acting through the legitimate compound interface `D→X`.

## 7. Required tests 4 and 5 — verified positive controls

### Test 4: ordinary LF adequacy for natural deduction

**VERIFIED_SOURCE:** Harper–Honsell–Plotkin Theorem 4.1 gives, for every first-order formula φ and the specified individual/hypothesis contexts (X,Δ), a bijection of valid raw proof expressions with canonical terms of `true(εφ)` in the **declared Σ_FOL**, and states simultaneous term/proof substitution compositionality. Its proof gives a structural decoding of canonical terms. Source proof identity here is raw syntax modulo α; target LF conversion supplies canonical representatives.

On precisely this interface, **N_split YES**: canonicalize and decode. On classes this inverse commutes with the theorem's simultaneous substitutions, discharge and parametric binders. **N_rep YES** on the associated substitution category.

Nevertheless ordinary LF does **not** meet the fixed-core derived-rule provenance obligation: `imp-i`, `imp-e`, `raa`, etc. are declared constants in Σ_FOL, not derivations from the bare LF core. It is an adequate D1 representation, not automatically a fundamental D2 core. The matrix distinguishes predicate acceptance from that separate failure; accepting faithful representation is not certifying its trusted machinery.

**Equality sensitivity:** keep the same encoding but demand object implication β equality. With x:true(p),

```text
imp-e p p (imp-i p p (λz.z)) x
```

has a constant head and does not LF-β-reduce to x. Hence this representation does not even induce the required map on the object-β quotient; neither criterion can certify it as such a faithful interface. That is a failure of the chosen encoding/equality pair. It is not a theorem that LF cannot represent that equality with different machinery. The criteria were not changed between these two input equality choices.

### Test 5: genuine Girard translation into linear logic

Use Hasegawa's **2000** Girard translation, rather than silently assuming that a CPS term translation preserves every ordinary hypothesis substitution. This supplies an especially explicit positive control.

**VERIFIED_SOURCE:** *Girard Translation and Logical Predicates*, JFP 10(1), 77–89, §§2–3 and Theorem 5.6. Source: STLC with the stated βη equations. Target: the specified dual-context linear λ-calculus with !, linear implication and the displayed βη/let/commuting equations. Its formulas and templates are

```text
b°=b;       (σ→τ)°=!σ°⊸τ°;
x°=x;       (λx.M)°=λy.let !x be y in M°;
(MN)°=M°(!N°);       Γ ↦ Γ°;∅.
```

Proposition 3.1 proves typing. Lemma 3.2 states `M°[N°/x]≡α(M[N/x])°`. Propositions 3.3–3.4 preserve and reflect equality. Theorem 5.6 states: for every source Γ,σ, every target proof `Γ°;∅⊢M:σ°` equals N° for some `Γ⊢N:σ`. There is no source-specific rule signature.

The inverse in Proposition 3.4 is explicit: erase ! on types/terms, turn linear arrows into ordinary arrows, and replace `let !x be M in N` by `N•[M•/x]`. It preserves typing/equality, and `(M°)•≡α M`. Structural induction gives substitution and binder compatibility. At the translated unrestricted contexts this is an effective structural retraction: **N_split YES**. **N_rep YES** as well. This genuinely derives source introduction/elimination within the target rather than checking serialized rule applications.

The source's contraction/weakening is exposed in the unrestricted Γ zone and the ! on arrow domains. The empty linear zone is part of the interface. ## 9. Refactoring attacks, restrictions and the next gate

### 9.1 What survives equivalent presentations?

**N_split and N_rep survive effective structured isomorphisms**, including transparent definitional extension/inlining and replacement of generators by an explicitly inverse generating presentation. For N_split, conjugate G by the isomorphisms. For N_rep, transport the representable diagram; alternatively apply Yoneda again. Substitution stability follows the retained typed structure. These are advantages as representation invariants, but they preserve the checker false positive too.

For a group presentation, adding `a=w` as a definitional generator or eliminating a redundant generator changes neither its group nor the retraction obstruction. A single compound derived word can hide arbitrarily much of the selected embedding, so counting generators or banning an operation called “eval” cannot remedy the matrix. Inlining the finite lookup for literal G likewise does not change the checker/path isomorphism. Freshly trusting its entries would instead change the ledger and fail the interface.

**D2 diagnostic:** native atom images and a source-independent wrapper reject the explicit indexed checker/path interfaces as written. But D2 accepts A_U's atom-to-atom word representation. That is not automatically a false positive: the permitted source has only group-word structure. D2 also excludes the perfectly faithful compound embedding of C_n into the free symmetric monoidal category by sending p to `X^⊗n` and rotation to an n-cycle. Its image uses n wires for one recorded slot; it does not violate resource conservation as a block. Thus native atomicity imposes a substantive representation restriction, not an invariant synonymous with mathematical meaning. Neither D2 nor its atom restriction is silently substituted for the tested criteria.

More seriously, the rule/assumption inverse in §2.4 shows that preserving the proof category does not preserve whether a primitive was assumed or derived. A provenance restriction can be mathematically well-defined **with an annotated source interface**, but cannot be described as invariant under all unannotated equivalent presentations. This qualification is necessary even for legitimate foundational axioms.

### 9.3 Failed approaches and outcome for ProofBasis

Reconstruction, even two-sided on classes, failed as non-vacuity. Representability, universal elements and probe naturality failed. Native atomicity distinguishes some presentations but rejects legitimate compound structural embeddings and leaves the group control intact. Rejecting universal groups merely because they can express difficult computation would reject actual group-theoretic structure without a stated mathematical reason. Global checking difficulty and nonconfluence were not used as impossibility claims.

The findings do **not** establish nonexistence of a finite universal proof algebra. They do not provide an existence construction for all C.

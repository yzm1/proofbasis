# ProofBasis: external programme review

**Date:** 2026-10-14. **Scope:** the whole repository from the charter (2026-10-08) through PR #40 and Task 019 (2026-10-14). Line references are to commit 53cd1dc unless stated. **Status:** an external review, not a theorem. §10 says how it was produced and what it cannot vouch for.

The owner asked four questions: is this worth pursuing, were mistakes made, do the conclusions and the direction make sense, and what comes next. Short answers first, evidence after.

## 1. Verdict

**Worth pursuing in its current form: no.** The question the charter asks was answered, at the level at which it is answerable, on the first day. At the derivability level the answer is "yes, trivially, and known since the 1980s": any universal framework does it (LF adequacy, rewriting-logic reflection, Mossakowski–Diaconescu–Tarlecki Prop. 2.25, Jeřábek 2012). At the proof-identity level, one algebra faithful to classical and intuitionistic proofs on a shared cartesian zone collapses under known theorems, but that is not what the charter demands: it allows per-source identities, and a target that keeps classical identity in a linear zone and intuitionistic identity in a cartesian zone escapes the collapse. The honest forms of the question are therefore a zoned proposition about one fixed framework (which is what the programme eventually froze as SC) or a doctrine-indexed family (which is the Licata–Shulman–Riley and Shulman programme). The repository's own day-one self-review proposed the generic-framework route and named the one discriminating test. That test was never run. Four working days and about 19,200 lines later, the programme accepted a routine corollary of cut elimination inside Shulman's framework and froze a next task that re-derives standard material.

**Mistakes: yes, but not false mathematics.** No false theorem survived. Sixty-four cited theorem statements were re-fetched from primary texts; all were accurate at theorem level, with half a dozen bibliographic slips. Ten recorded PDF hashes were re-checked and all match. The mistakes are of direction, process and emphasis: the decisive test deferred three times; definition rounds that produced controls but no proposition; "independent review" by two instances of one model family accepted ten minutes after the last review; a routine result framed as a milestone with its direct template uncited; four items declared open that close from models and theorems the repository already had; and a governance layer that recorded proposals but never a decision.

**Do the conclusions make sense?** The negative ones do: the original formulation is ill-posed; derivability is subsumed; an interface-level "not a checker" predicate is not definable; the honest forms are zoned or indexed. The positive conclusion (the atomic result) is true and carries no information about the charter. The direction (Task 019) does not make sense as sized: its equality-reflection half closes in a few pages from standard models, its fullness half is the lemma already accepted plus a subformula argument, and neither half bears on "diverse foundations". §7 gives the detail.

**Recommendation:** stop the agent loop now, retire the charter sentence as the north star, and spend two weeks on the four cheap steps all three advocates in this review agreed on: reconcile the ledger, state the one general lemma on two pages and send it to three named researchers, type-check the displayed derivations, and get one proof theorist outside the loop to read the lemma. Let the day-14 answer decide between a short note in Shulman's programme and an honest archive. Both are successes by the charter's own criteria. Running Task 019 as written is not. §8 has the plan.

## 2. What you set out to do

The charter asks whether a fixed, finite set of mathematically meaningful proof-construction operations can represent finite formal proofs across significantly different logical foundations, with assumptions kept in a separate environment, while preserving composition, substitution and binding, resource discipline, a chosen notion of proof identity, and causal order; and not merely by encoding a proof checker. It counts a known subsuming theorem, a decisive obstruction, or a defensible narrowed conjecture as success, and it pre-registers that the requirements may be jointly inconsistent. That framing is careful and honest.

Two things in it had to be decided before mathematics could start. First, which class of foundations: the candidate conjecture quantifies the class existentially, which makes it satisfiable by a one-element class. Second, what "not a checker" means as a predicate. Neither was ever decided by the owner. Both were fixed downstream by agent choices (a source class in report 018; two fragments in SC; explicit doctrine hypotheses in place of a predicate) that no decision-log entry records. A third requirement, causal order, was parked at Stage 0.7 into an optional extension and never revisited; it is the one requirement that distinguished the project from standard logical-framework adequacy.

### Glossary

- **NJ βη:** intuitionistic natural deduction with products and implication, proofs identified up to β and η; the free cartesian closed category on the atoms.
- **MLL nets:** cut-free, unit-free multiplicative linear logic without the MIX rule, proofs identified by their axiom linking, equivalently up to rule commutation.
- **Doctrine, D\*:** a chosen menu of connectives each defined by a universal property. D\* is Shulman's two-zone menu: nonlinear terminal, product, internal hom; linear tensor, par, unit, counit, dual; and an adjunction F ⊣ U between the zones. A\*(X,Y) is the free structure on nonlinear atoms X and linear atoms Y; E\* is its full equational theory.
- **SC:** the frozen conjecture that NJ βη and MLL nets sit inside A\*(X,Y) at their own boundaries with no new proofs (fullness, reflection) and no new equalities (equality reflection), and that the embedding respects composition and substitution.
- **Fullness vs equality reflection:** fullness says every target proof at a source boundary comes from a source proof; equality reflection says two source proofs identified in the target were already equal in the source.
- **Atomic result:** every proof p → p in A\*(∅,{p}) equals the identity.
- **LSR Conjecture 8.5:** the lemma Licata, Shulman and Riley need for their own adequacy template: equal derivations in their generic framework have permutation-equivalent normal forms.

## 4. What was actually established

Graded by the repository's own vocabulary after independent reconstruction.

**Established by prior art, verified from primary texts.** Derivability-level universality of a fixed finite framework (Clavel–Meseguer Thm 3.2; Mossakowski–Diaconescu–Tarlecki Prop. 2.25; Jeřábek 2012 Thm 2.4; LF adequacy, Harper–Honsell–Plotkin Thms 4.1 and 4.2). Collapse constraints on proof identity under their stated hypotheses (Joyal's lemma via Došen Props. 1–3, which needs an initial object and natural double-negation elimination; Selinger Cor. 3.8, which needs a bifunctorial par; Došen's maximality). Unit-free MLL net freeness (Heijltjes–Straßburger Thm 5.10) and rule-commutation identity (van Glabbeek–Hughes Thm 1). Free completion and sequent presentation for LNL doctrines (Shulman 2023 Thm 7.4 and Prop. 8.3), with cut elimination for the generic doctrine calculus explicitly left open by its author. Star-autonomous conservativity (Shulman 2021 Thm 10.1).

**Correct and elementary, produced in-repo.** The K_dec refutation (a many-one reduction; a Thue system would do in place of a group; it had real decision value because decidable target equality was live in candidates in reports 001, 008, 010 and 014). The atomic-profile pigeonhole in 005. The OI lemma (an isomorphism-invariant predicate agrees on isomorphic interfaces; a conjugation argument the repository itself labels elementary). The controls built along the way (the universal-group target, the reader and Cayley wrappers, the k-extension, the constant-atom table, the MLL fixture) are correct and were reused.

**Informal, true and routine: the atomic result.** Two verifiers here reconstructed report 023's cut/substitution argument against Shulman's definitions and found it sound with presentational gaps only; one of them spent its effort trying to refute the statement and could not, since every candidate term cancels under the frozen equations (confidence about 0.97). A third verifier checked prior art. The statement is the expected first corollary of equality-sound cut elimination plus the subformula property for a classical two-zone LNL calculus. Benton 1994 Theorems 24 and 25 give the intuitionistic template, including the n-ary cut device that report 025 treats as a design necessity without noting Benton had it; Shulman's cited page says his F and U rules specialize to Benton's. No published theorem covers the exact classical doctrine with a native nonlinear implication, so "routine assembly" is the right description, not "existing theorem". None of reports 022–028 cites Benton or any cut-elimination result. The adversarial agent said the outcome was expected (024:184) and the task design presupposed it. No referee would count the statement as a contribution.

**Closable from theorems the repository already cites, but listed as open.** Three closures were checked adversarially here and survived; they are informal consequences of published results, at the same epistemic level as the repository's own arguments.

- NJ-side equality reflection and derivability reflection. Any cartesian closed category together with the terminal category as linear part is a full D\*-model (every clause of Shulman Defs. 2.1 to 2.15 checked), so the free structure retracts onto the free CCC; the canonical map is injective on hom-sets, and every target inhabitant at a pure nonlinear boundary yields a source proof. This holds modulo the unit-conservativity step the repository already assumes at 017:89. It does not give NJ-side fullness. The repository says a model-extension theorem would still be required (020:106) and lists both items as live falsifiers (021:26-29; 028:62-63).
- MLL-side equality reflection. Sets and relations, with the graph functor left adjoint to powerset, form a full D\*-model (Shulman Prop. 3.15(iii)); distinct axiom linkings on the same forest have distinct relational interpretations once atoms have two elements. This is a one-paragraph folklore argument, enumeration-checked here up to eight leaves. The repository is one step away: it built the FinSet and finite-vector-space model (019 §5.3; 026 §5) and used it for a single pair. One correction for any resized task: the sentinel "⊢ p⊥, p⊥, p, p" is a MIX-only sequent; the repository's own sentinel at 019:199 is the right one.
- The unit-extension identification and linear equality reflection on the pure completion (022:148-150; 022:226-227). The first closes from Shulman 2023 Thm 7.4 and Prop. 5.8 with Shulman 2021 Thm 10.1; the second more simply from the envelope embedding (2021 Thms 6.6 and 7.5), with a size footnote. Report 022 cites the papers and Thm 10.1 but not the envelope theorems that do the work.

**Closable if the accepted lemma holds: fullness at pure boundaries.** The auxiliary calculus of report 023 §3 has the subformula property (checked rule by rule), so a cut-free derivation of a translated pure sequent uses only pure rules, which are the images of the source rules. Fullness at all translated pure boundaries is then a one-to-three-page corollary of the lemma accepted in 028, within the accepted scope of one linear atom, and needs an atom-generic restatement beyond it. It is conditional on that lemma, which currently has exactly one LLM reviewer, and this review did not certify it either. RESEARCH_PLAN.md:83 asks for this corollary as the next gate.

**Genuinely open, and the only content of SC.** Equality-sound cut elimination for Shulman's §8 calculus with the chosen cones, stated at full breadth (the standard cone menu on the five bases, not every finite discrete cone, since cut elimination can fail for bad cones) and checked by someone outside the loop. Shulman's open item is the generic doctrine calculus; the fixed-doctrine instance in 023 does not by itself convert into a citable contribution. Written as the general theorem, it would be a modest, citable note. The SC corollary alone would not carry a paper.

**Unresolved in the literature.** LSR Conjecture 8.5. Report 002's notes swept about 60 citing works on 10-08; this review's source verifier swept 59 Semantic Scholar and 32 OpenAlex citing works, overlapping, and found no proof or refutation; the manuscript is unchanged since June 2017. Not checked by anyone: Riley's 2022 thesis and Licata's 2019 slides; Google Scholar was inaccessible. Clarke–Scherer–Zeilberger (arXiv:2511.07314, 2025) is a unary analogue, not a resolution; it does not appear among the LSR citers, so citation sweeps miss it.

**One internal inconsistency in the acceptance record.** 028:56 leaves an "indirect compact scalar" open, but the accepted lemma already forces every endomorphism of the unit to be the identity, and the relevant hom-set is empty. Small, but it is the one place the record contradicts itself.

## 7. Does the current direction make sense?

Task 019 asks Agent A to establish, for arbitrary atoms and all boundaries, recovery and equation preservation, and Agent B to establish equality reflection, with reciprocal review. On this review's findings, Agent B's items 1 and 2 are the degenerate CCC model and the relational model, one step from the model the repository built in 019 §5.3 and 026 §5; Agent A's items 1 and 2 are the lemma accepted in 028 restated with atoms, plus the subformula argument. Running Task 019 as written would spend two more agent rounds, under the process in §5 item 2, confirming what can be written down today. One structural fact matters for any resizing: the degenerate-model trick provably fails on the linear side (the adjunction would force the unit's hom-sets to be singletons), so all genuine difficulty lives in linear-side fullness and NJ-side fullness, and both are the cut lemma.

Even completed, SC says that Benton's 1994 adjunction, in Shulman's classical variant, is conservative over its two generating fragments with proof identity. Every verifier here judged that specialists would expect this; the doctrine contains both fragments by stipulation (017:188-189 concedes it). It says nothing about dependent types, set theory, cyclic validity, trust boundaries or causal order. A negative outcome (a modal or unit detour identifying two distinct nets, or a mixed proof with no pure inhabitant) would be more citable than the positive one, and nobody expects it.

## 10. How this review was done, and its limits

Sixteen reviewers read the repository in parallel: eight stage readers, three mathematical verifiers on the atomic result (soundness reconstruction, refutation attempt, prior-art check), three primary-source verifiers (64 cited theorem statements re-fetched; ten recorded hashes matched), one methodology critic (git and GitHub timestamps, PR reviews, authorship), and one well-posedness analysis of the charter against the literature. Thirteen material findings, including every new mathematical claim the reviewers made against the repository, were then handed to separate adversarial refuters; findings are reported with the refuters' corrections. Three advocates argued stop, continue and pivot; a judge scored them; a completeness critic audited the draft of this document against the evidence files and its corrections were applied.

Limits, stated plainly because they mirror the repository's. Every reviewer, refuter, advocate, judge and critic was a Claude instance: this review has the single-vendor property it criticises in the repository's Codex phase, mitigated by primary-source fetching and adversarial pairing, not by vendor diversity. Refuters were not blind; each was handed the allegation it was to defeat. The whole review ran in about one hour of wall-clock with up to sixteen agents in parallel; the difference from the ten-minute acceptance it criticises is that every claim here is pinned to a file and line and can be audited by grep. No mathematician outside the loop has read the repository or this review. The three closures in §4 are informal arguments by LLMs, unmechanized except for an eight-leaf enumeration; the product-type extension cites a source not inspected; one closure carries a size footnote; the fullness corollary is conditional on an unverified lemma. This review could not access Girard's Logic of Unity, Lambek–Scott p. 67, Iemhoff 2001, Riley's thesis, Licata's slides, the full texts of Hyland–Schalk, Maietti et al., Squier–Otto–Kobayashi or Cockett–Seely, Hasegawa 2000's theorem numbers, or Google Scholar; Girard 1987 was read from partly garbled OCR and Clavel–Meseguer from decoded PostScript. Who the coordinator is could not be determined; the process findings are about the record, not about what the owner read. The judgement that the atomic result and SC are routine is a consensus of agents who fetched and read Benton, Shulman, Hasegawa and the coherence literature; a working categorical proof theorist could overturn it in an afternoon, and that afternoon is the single most valuable thing the owner can buy.

# Cross-theory comparison rubric

Use this rubric for every candidate framework, not merely a narrative overview.

1. **Mathematical object:** Exact definitions and sorts of objects, judgments, transformations and morphisms.
2. **Composition/generation:** Primitive operations, derived operations, substitution, binders and equations; what is finitely generated and by which schema.
3. **Foundational neutrality:** Which assumptions enter as parameters, axioms, rules, interpretations, or trusted computation.
4. **Fidelity:** Distinguish soundness, preservation, reflection, fullness, faithful equality, structural correspondence, causal independence, higher coherence.
5. **Presentation changes:** Notion of equivalence, translation, Morita/term equivalence, and what is invariant under generator changes.
6. **Justification:** What makes operations logical/sound/admissible/harmonious? Is validity external or intrinsic?
7. **Graph/time:** What is dependency vs scheduling, independence vs conflict, event histories vs proof equality?
8. **Finiteness and limits:** Finite generators vs schemas vs finite objects; undecidability, complexity, completeness boundaries.
9. **Baseline controls:** NJ βη, classical, linear, dependent binding, rewrite, cyclic, proof-net permutation, checker interpreter, group/presentation.
10. **Usefulness and risks:** Exact theorem that would advance the north star, counterexample that threatens it, integration work required, expected decision value.

**Evidence row required for any decisive claim:** precise source URL/DOI and inspected theorem location, status, assumptions, result, scope limitation, and strongest objection.

Do not score a framework 'universal' solely because it encodes proofs.

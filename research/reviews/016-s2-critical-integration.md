## Audit findings (critical, not consensus by averaging)

**C1 — Reflection is currently underspecified.** CMP-01 describes R as all target inhabitants at a translated boundary implying a source derivation, but does not uniformly specify whether target contexts, judgment wrappers, image predicates, signature extensions, or computation rules are permitted. Permitting image-specific restrictions can trivialize reflection; forbidding them can exclude standard CPS and relative interpretations. **Repair:** define two *named* contracts: intrinsic boundary reflection, and relative/image reflection; only the former may count as a direct stronger explanatory witness. Do not silently transfer a theorem between them.

**C2 — Trust/core boundary is a ledger, not yet an invariant.** Recording object rule imports in Env/Prov is essential but a declaration can be moved into type constructors, parameters, or a closed encoding. Reports 009, 010, 014 and 015 demonstrate aspects. **Repair:** describe an explicitly parameterized doctrine and a morphism of doctrines. Compare derived operations relative to the same designated base rather than trying to characterize 'true reasoning' from proof-class isomorphism.

**C3 — Equality changes across descriptions.** ALG-01 relies on a beta/eta quotient for a CCC; INST-01 discusses raw LF adequacy and specific mode equations; OBS-01 invokes proof-net equivalence. These are not instances of a single comparison without a table of chosen Eq and a proof that translation respects/refects exactly it. **Repair:** use two contrasting concrete source examples, one NJ beta/eta and one MLL permutation/net quotient, with separate source and target equality generators.

**C4 — One negative example overstates what was shown.** Naive linear-to-Cartesian mapping fails R/U, but it establishes only failure of that particular translation, not incompatibility of linear and cartesian categorical semantics generally. The reports correctly acknowledge this limitation. Retain it as a negative control, not a selection criterion that prejudges modal/LNL encodings.

**C5 — Finiteness claims depend on dimension and doctrine.** Squier FDT concerns specified monoid rewriting presentations/coherence, Rybakov admissibility concerns the closure of IPC admissible rules, and target decidable equality obstructions depend on effective EP/ER translations. They cannot be combined into an impossibility of a single finite proof algebra without a transport theorem and matching quantifiers. No such transport theorem appears in these reports.

**C6 — Higher and causal structures remain optional but not disposable.** CMP-01 provides fields CF/HC but no worked comparison that simultaneously preserves Eq and event structure through erasure/duplication. The structural data may be presentation-relative. Mark it unknown rather than N/A where the original north star motivates it.

**C7 — Independent primary-source verification remains incomplete.** ALG-01 and OBS-01 explicitly rely on abstracts/excerpts for parts of Squier, second-order equivalences, and IPC admissibility. INST-01 cannot resolve current LSR Conjecture 8.5 status. A new existence conjecture depending on these statements must verify exact theorem texts and current bibliography; a missing paper is not a proof a conjecture remains open.

**C8 — 'Finite' has at least seven non-equivalent definitions** in OBS-01; before S3 require a vector of finiteness claims rather than calling a fixed grammar a finite universal proof basis.

## Reconciliation: what survives

1. There are meaningful finite rule/equation schemas for particular proof theories, with established categorical or algebraic semantics.
2. Mode- and doctrine-indexed generic frameworks can provide common composition/resource machinery relative to declared structural parameters.
3. A generative syntax, a proof-theoretic justification, and a faithful representation theorem are distinct kinds of mathematical output.
4. No reviewed report establishes one finite, source-independent explanation of arbitrary proof operations or proves it impossible.
5. Presentation change, especially adding defined generators or transporting data into types, can defeat syntactic purity criteria.
